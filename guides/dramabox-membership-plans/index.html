DramaBox Membership Plans: What $119.99 a Year Actually Buys

By marluola Published 25 October 2026 Updated 25 October 2026 Prices checked in-app on 2026-10-09
Short answer
  • DramaBox sells two membership plans. Weekly Membership is $9.99 a week for the first three weeks (shown as 23% off the $12.99 list price), then renews at $12.99 a week. Annual Membership is $119.99 a year.
  • The app markets the annual plan as "Only $2.31/week", and the arithmetic is right: $119.99 divided by 52 weeks is $2.3075.
  • So the same membership screen quotes you two different weekly prices: $12.99 to renew weekly, $2.31 on the annual plan — a 5.6x gap, and nothing on the screen explains that the cheaper number is not a weekly tier.
  • Neither plan lists different benefits. We could not find any screen separating what the weekly tier gets from what the annual tier gets — the difference is price and commitment only.
  • If you keep it longer than about week 10, the annual plan has already cost you less than weekly renewals would have, and it saves roughly $555 over a full year.

What we ran into while testing

  • The membership screen shows two different weekly numbers at the same time — $12.99 on the weekly row and "Only $2.31/week" on the annual row. Nothing on the screen says the second one is an annual price divided up.
  • A $3.00-off coupon popup was stacked on top of the $9.99 intro price, with its own 23:59 countdown. The price you actually pay on day one is therefore lower than the plan row, and that floor moves.
  • The homepage VIP entry carried a -23% tag on the same day the subscription page showed 23% off. Both apps rotate their discount daily, so we would not expect the two to keep agreeing.
  • We went looking for a screen that lists what weekly members get versus what annual members get, and there is not one. Both tiers sit under a single benefit list — the difference is price and commitment, not features.
  • Member points are labelled "Points are for paid members only", and 500 points buy one extra day of membership. The refund arrives in time, not in money.

The two membership plans

DramaBox sells access to its catalogue in two ways, and the membership tier list is short enough to fit on one screen: Weekly Membership and Annual Membership. The plan list we recorded had two rows in it — no monthly option, no middle tier, no family or student plan.

What separates them, as far as we could find, is price and nothing else. Both tiers sit under one benefit list: 16,000+ series with 100+ new dramas a week, downloads, 1080p playback, members-only titles, daily member points, and three free series gifts a week to friends. We went looking for a screen that splits the two entitlements and did not find one.

Every price below was read inside the app on an Android phone with a US Google Play account on 9 October 2026, all in USD. We also captured the same pages in Japanese yen a day earlier, which turns out to matter — see the regional pricing section.

Weekly Membership: three cheap weeks

The weekly plan is the cheap entry point and the trap in the same screen.

  • Intro: $9.99 a week for the first three weeks, shown against a crossed-out $12.99, labelled 23% off, with a 24-hour countdown
  • Renewal: $12.99 a week from week four
  • A separate $3.00-off coupon popup appeared on top of the intro on the same day, with its own 23:59 limit

Three things about that opening number:

  1. $9.99 is not the floor. The $3.00 coupon stacked on top of it meant the effective first week was $6.99 on the day we tested. Those stackable coupons rotate, so treat any single week as a snapshot rather than a quote.
  2. The renewal rate is 30% above the intro. $12.99 against $9.99. Over a year of weekly renewals, that gap is where the money goes.
  3. The countdown sits on the paywall, not only on the plan row. The app’s own help text says Google Play charges automatically within 24 hours of each period ending, and the cancel button lives in Google Play — see how to cancel a DramaBox subscription for the mechanics.

Annual Membership: $119.99, sold as $2.31 a week

The annual plan is $119.99 for the year, and the app does the division for you: “Only $2.31/week”.

That number is arithmetically honest. $119.99 divided by 52 weeks is $2.3075, rounded to $2.31. Nothing is hidden inside it — it is the annual price spread over 52 weeks and nothing else.

The awkward part is what it sits next to. On the same membership screen, the weekly tier’s own renewal rate is $12.99 a week. One of those two numbers is a weekly price you can actually be charged. The other is an annual price divided up.

The two weekly numbers on one screen

This is the most confusing thing about DramaBox’s membership screen, and it is worth saying plainly:

The app quotes you two different weekly prices for the same product.
NumberWhere it comes fromWhat it actually is
$12.99/weekThe weekly tier’s renewal rateA real weekly charge
$2.31/weekThe annual plan divided by 52An annual charge, spread out

$12.99 divided by $2.31 is 5.6. The same app, the same catalogue, the same word “week” on screen, a 5.6x difference — and nothing on the screen tells you that the cheaper number is not a weekly tier.

What that means in practice:

  • If you will not watch for a full year, $2.31 a week is not a price you can have. It is not a discount tier. It is the annual plan amortised.
  • If you would have paid weekly anyway, the annual plan is worth roughly $555 over a year. $12.99 times 52 weeks is $675.48, minus $119.99.
  • The break-even is about week 10. Three weeks at $9.99 costs $29.97, and every week after that is $12.99. Cumulative spend passes $119.99 in week ten — so if you expect to stay past ten weeks, the annual plan has already won.

And there is a second-order effect worth noticing: a weekly-only subscriber never sees the annual price as a discount. They see $12.99 on their row and $119.99 on a different row, with no percentage-off badge and no comparison anywhere. If you are on the weekly tier and intend to stay past week ten, the app is not going to prompt you.

Why 'membership plans' is the right thing to search for

Plenty of pages will quote you DramaBox’s price. Fewer will tell you that the price you are looking at depends on which of two weekly numbers the screen happens to be showing you — and that the annual tier has no percentage-off badge to make the saving visible. That gap is what this page is for.

What $119.99 a year actually includes

From the app’s own benefits list:

  • 16,000+ series, unlimited
  • 100+ new dramas a week
  • Downloads for offline viewing
  • 1080p playback
  • Members-only titles
  • Member points, credited daily
  • Three free series gifts a week to friends

Two of those deserve a second look.

The 100+ new dramas a week is what makes the annual plan defensible for DramaBox specifically. A $2.31-a-week unlock only holds if there is enough new material to justify it, and 100+ new titles a week is the highest refresh rate we measured across the six apps we opened.

Three free series gifts a week is the one benefit that reads as a growth mechanism rather than a perk. It costs DramaBox nothing directly and pulls three people into the app every week. Treat it as a feature of the plan, not a gift to you.

Member points are a retention hook, not a bonus

Member points can only be earned by paying members — the app says so on the screen — and 500 points buy one extra day of membership, 1,000 buy five. It is a partial refund in time on a subscription you have already paid for. Worth taking, but it should not factor into whether you subscribe in the first place.

When coins beat both plans

The third route is not a membership plan at all. Coins unlock individual episodes at 50 coins each, and for some people they beat both tiers.

The free route tops the balance up from three places: daily check-ins (10 coins rising to 50 on day seven, 165 for a full week), 15 video tasks a day at 20 coins each (300 a day), and 8 ad unlocks a day.

Where the arithmetic lands. The first DramaBox series we opened was 43 episodes with the paywall at episode 12, so finishing it costs 32 episodes times 50 coins — 1,600 coins, which is about $16 from the smallest pack or about $11 from the largest.

Which means the annual plan has to earn its keep across roughly eight to eleven series a year. $119.99 against $15.95 is about 7.5 series; against $11.42 it is about 10.5. Below that range, coins are simply cheaper. Above it, the annual plan wins by a wide margin.

That is the real shape of the decision, and none of it is visible from the membership screen — the app never tells you how many series a year you need to break even on an annual plan. We work the per-episode side of it out in how many coins per episode on DramaBox.

Two smaller details worth knowing: when you unlock an episode the app spends reward coins first, so collected free coins get used before paid ones. And DramaBox coins price out slightly cheaper per episode than ReelShort’s, which charges 60 coins from a $4.99/500 pack — we break that comparison down in ReelShort cost per episode.

DramaBox is the cheapest weekly tier. $12.99 on renewal, against $19.99 for GoodShort and ShortMax and $19.99 for ReelShort’s top tier — a $7-a-week gap against two of them. If you plan to subscribe for a few weeks and then cancel, DramaBox’s weekly tier is the one to look at.

Your plan price depends on your connection

DramaBox prices by the country your connection appears to come from, not by your store account.

On our test device the same account showed Japanese yen through one network path and US dollars through another, one day apart. The app’s own store help text says: “The current product prices are displayed in USD. Please restart DramaBox to refresh the localized pricing.”

The yen figures we captured for the same coin packs on the same day: 300 coins at ¥410, 770 coins at ¥1,060, 1,600 coins at ¥1,960 and 3,500 coins at ¥3,760.

Practically: before you compare DramaBox’s price against any other site, confirm the currency on your own screen matches the article you are reading. A yen price is not a dollar price, and a review that mixes them is wrong on arrival.

What this page does not cover

  • When this breakdown does not work for you: if you are outside the US, or if your connection routes through a different country, every figure above is wrong for your screen. And if you only want one series badly, this page is the wrong tool — coins at the smallest pack are about $11 to $16 for a full series, against $119.99 for a year.
  • We did not test iOS pricing. Apple’s commission sometimes produces different in-app prices, and we will not claim a number we have not seen.
  • We could not find a benefits screen separating the two tiers. Our statement that weekly and annual members get the same entitlements is based on both tiers sitting under one benefit list and on the absence of any screen that separates them. If the app varies entitlements behind the join screen, we did not see it.
  • The per-episode coin maths assumes 50 coins per episode, which held across the titles we opened. Series length and promotional pricing vary widely, so per-series figures are worked examples rather than a guarantee.
  • This page does not tell you whether DramaBox is worth watching — only what its membership plans cost and what they include. For that, see our full DramaBox review, including where its paywall lands compared with ReelShort.

Bottom line

Two plans, and they are not two versions of the same price:

  • Weekly Membership — $9.99 for three weeks, then $12.99 a week. The cheapest weekly tier of the six apps we measured.
  • Annual Membership — $119.99, which the app sells as “$2.31/week”. The arithmetic is correct, but it is an annual price spread over 52 weeks, not a weekly rate you can buy.

The one thing to carry out of here: on DramaBox’s own membership screen, “$2.31 a week” and “$12.99 a week” are both labelled “a week”, and they differ by 5.6x. Only one of them is a weekly charge.

If you will be here past week 10, take the annual plan — you have already spent more than $119.99 by then, and it saves roughly $555 across a full year. If you are finishing one series and moving on, buy coins at the smallest pack and skip both; see the full DramaBox cost breakdown and the free coin routes.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a DramaBox membership?

In our in-app test on Android (9 October 2026), Weekly Membership was $9.99 a week for the first three weeks, displayed as 23% off the $12.99 list price, and then renewed at $12.99 a week. Annual Membership was $119.99 for the year. A $3.00-off coupon popup was stacked on the intro price that day, so $9.99 is not the floor — treat it as a snapshot.

What is the DramaBox yearly subscription discount?

There is no percentage-off badge on the annual plan. The app divides $119.99 by 52 weeks and shows "Only $2.31/week" instead. Compared with renewing the weekly tier at $12.99 for 52 weeks, which comes to $675.48, the annual plan is about $555 cheaper. The discount is real, but it is presented as a weekly rate rather than as a saving.

Is the DramaBox annual plan worth $119.99?

It depends entirely on how many series you finish. Finishing one series costs roughly $11 to $16 in coins, so the annual plan needs about eight to eleven series a year to come out ahead. Below that, coins are cheaper. Past about week ten, the annual plan is already ahead of weekly renewals.

Can you watch everything with a DramaBox membership?

Membership unlocks the whole catalogue without coins: 16,000+ series with 100+ new dramas added every week, plus downloads, 1080p playback, member-only titles, daily member points and three free series gifts a week to friends. Coin unlocking and ads still appear on the paywall — membership removes the need to pay per episode, not the presence of the offer.

Do weekly and annual members get the same things?

As far as we could find, yes. Both tiers sit under one benefit list, and we could not find a screen that lists different entitlements for the weekly and annual plans. The difference is the price and how long you are committed — not the catalogue.
