The short answer

These two apps are much closer than the price tags suggest, and the closeness is the useful finding. Both charge 50 coins per locked episode — identical, episode for episode. If you buy coins, you are buying the same thing on either app.

Where they diverge is the subscription. DramaBox sells weekly access at $9.99 for three weeks and $12.99 after that, with an annual pass at $119.99. GoodShort charges $19.99 a week and $149.99 a year. That is a $30 gap on the annual pass and $7 a week — and it is the whole reason to choose between them.

One honest caveat before the tables

GoodShort’s promotional popup reads “US$ 19.99/w for 3 weeks, then /w” — and the renewal price is simply missing from that sentence. We photographed it (see below) and could not resolve it. So on GoodShort we can only confirm the first price. Everything below treats $19.99 as the ongoing weekly figure, which is the worst-case assumption.

Both apps label their bonus percentages openly, and GoodShort is arguably cleaner about it: its packs carry explicit +5%, +10% and +35% badges.

The real difference is not the coins

If you are deciding between these two apps on the strength of a per-episode table, the table is telling you they are the same. They are. Pick on the subscription price and the free tier instead.

The two passes are the same product at different prices, so the honest way to judge them is against what you would otherwise spend. Finishing series with coins costs roughly $9.99–$24.99 each on DramaBox and $15.92–$29.99 on GoodShort — so GoodShort’s $149.99 annual pass only makes sense once you would have spent more than that on coins, which for GoodShort means roughly five or more series.

DramaBox reaches that point sooner. On the same arithmetic its $119.99 is matched by about five series in coins, and it keeps being the cheaper option for everything above that.

Why neither of these annual passes is cheap

$119.99 for a year of unlimited viewing is about $0.33 a day. Most streaming services cost more than that, so DramaBox is not expensive against Netflix standards. It is expensive against finishing one series — which is the comparison that actually matters here, and the one we make in our DramaBox price breakdown.

GoodShort takes the one category it wins: check-ins pay 320 coins a week against DramaBox’s 165 — nearly double, rising to 90 on day seven. If you are the sort of person who opens the app every day anyway, that difference is worth real money.

Two GoodShort caveats that cut the other way. Its ad unlocks cap at 5 a day, half of DramaBox’s 8, and we captured an “Ad unlock failed. Please try again later” message — so the free allowance there is both smaller and less reliable.

GoodShort's free coins come from a third party

Its reward system pays through offerwalls — the kind that ask you to install other apps or complete sponsored offers. That is a materially different thing from a first-party daily check-in, and it is why we would not count those coins as reliable income the way we count DramaBox’s.

The price we could not verify

We would rather flag this than paper over it. GoodShort’s promotional popup reads:

“US$ 19.99/w for 3 weeks, then /w”

The renewal figure after “then” is blank. We photographed the screen and could not resolve it, and GoodShort’s listing does not publish it either.

Assume you pay the promo price

Because we could not confirm a lower renewal, every GoodShort figure on this page uses $19.99 as the ongoing weekly price — the worst case for GoodShort. If its renewal turns out to be lower, DramaBox’s advantage is smaller than shown, never larger.

The same app also shows inconsistent discount claims — the home screen advertises “50% OFF” while the in-show popup says “51% OFF” for the same period. Neither percentage is verifiable because the struck-through base price is not displayed. Treat both as marketing rather than arithmetic.

Which one to pick

In order of how likely each factor is to decide it for you:

  1. You will subscribe — pick DramaBox. $30 cheaper a year, $7 cheaper a week. There is no version of this where GoodShort wins on price.

  2. You will buy coins to finish one series — pick DramaBox. $12.99 against $19.99, and a shorter paywall to get past.

  3. You open the app daily and like collecting — consider GoodShort. Its 320-coin check-in week is the one genuine advantage it holds. Pair it with the knowledge that its ad unlocks are capped at five and sometimes fail.

  4. You want the biggest free catalogue — pick DramaBox. 16,000+ claimed titles against GoodShort’s smaller library, and we have not measured GoodShort’s catalogue depth directly.

What this comparison cannot tell you

  • GoodShort’s renewal price is unconfirmed. Everything here uses the promotional $19.99 as the ongoing figure. If the real renewal is lower, the gap narrows.

  • One series per app. Our free-episode counts come from a 43-episode title on DramaBox and a 50-episode title on GoodShort. Neither app publishes its free block and both move by title, so treat these as samples.

  • Android only, October 2026. Both were tested through Google Play on an emulator with real billing. We have not verified iOS pricing.

  • Catalogue depth is not measured. DramaBox claims 16,000+ titles; we did not count GoodShort’s.

  • Content quality is subjective and mostly untested. We are a price comparison. Where the apps differ on dubbing quality or originals, we have not done the testing to tell you which is better.

Bottom line

Per episode, they are the same app. Fifty coins each, $0.50 on the smallest pack, and within four cents of each other at the best rate. Nothing here justifies switching for the coins.

Per subscription, DramaBox wins clearly. $119.99 against $149.99 a year — a $30 gap — and $12.99 against $19.99 a week. Same unlimited model, materially different price.

GoodShort’s one real advantage is its check-in, at 320 coins a week against DramaBox’s 165. If you collect daily it is worth having. It is offset by a shorter free block (7 episodes against 11) and ad unlocks that cap at five and sometimes fail outright.

So: subscribing or finishing one series, take DramaBox. Collecting daily streaks and nothing else, GoodShort is defensible. And check the renewal price in-app before you commit to either — we could not confirm GoodShort’s, and neither can you from the app’s own text.

Frequently asked questions

Is GoodShort cheaper than DramaBox?

No. GoodShort costs $19.99 a week and $149.99 a year, against DramaBox at $9.99 a week for three weeks then $12.99, and $119.99 a year. GoodShort's coin packs are almost identical to DramaBox's — both charge 50 coins per episode, and the best rate is $0.36 on DramaBox against $0.37 on GoodShort. DramaBox is cheaper on subscriptions and effectively equal on coins.

How much is one episode on DramaBox vs GoodShort?

Exactly the same coin price: 50 coins on both. What differs is what a coin costs, which depends on the pack. On the cheapest pack both are $0.50 per episode. On the largest pack DramaBox works out at $0.36 and GoodShort at $0.37. Because the coin price is identical, the episode price is nearly identical too — the difference between these two apps is in the subscriptions, not the coins.

Which app has more free episodes?

DramaBox, in our testing. Its series was free from episode 1 and hit the paywall at episode 12 — about 11 free episodes. GoodShort was free from episode 1 to episode 7 before locking, about 7. Neither app publishes its free block and both vary by title, so treat these as measured samples rather than fixed rules.

Can I cancel a GoodShort subscription?

As with every app in this category, cancelling goes through the store you subscribed on — Google Play or the App Store — rather than inside the app. GoodShort's own listing says "Cancel anytime". One caveat specific to GoodShort: we could not confirm what it renews at after the promotional weeks, because the figure is missing from its promo text.

Is GoodShort worth it over DramaBox?

Only for one specific reason: its check-in pays 320 coins across seven days against DramaBox's 165. If you open the app daily and collect them, that gap is real money. In exchange you pay $30 more a year and get fewer free episodes before the paywall. For most people finishing one or two series, DramaBox is the better buy.
