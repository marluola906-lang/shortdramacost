Is StardustTV Free? What You Get Before It Asks for Money

By marluola Published 18 October 2026 Updated 18 October 2026 Prices checked in-app on 2026-10-09
Short answer
  • Yes, it is free to download and free to start watching. We opened a series, watched 10 episodes without paying anything, and hit the paywall at episode 11 of a 50-episode series.
  • What "free" means here: 10 free episodes, a 6-day daily sign-in chain worth 270 coins, and 5 ad-unlocks a day (each ad unlocks 5 episodes).
  • After that it costs 65 coins per episode — about $0.65 on the smallest pack. The free window is 20% of a series.
  • It also supports PayPal, which is unusual for this category and means you are not locked into Google Play billing.
  • The catch nobody mentions: three different weekly prices coexist on the same screen ($19.99, $17.99, $13.19), so the price you are quoted depends on which offer loads.
  • It is also the only app of the six we tested that labels its content "Contains AI-generated content" on the playback screen.

What we ran into while testing

  • StardustTV gave 10 free episodes of a 50-episode drama and locked from episode 11 — the most generous free window we measured.
  • StardustTV unlocks five episodes a day, but each one costs two ads. The daily total looks generous until you count the ads.
  • StardustTV pays for reading. Read three news articles for 10 coins, open two pages for 10 more. It is the only app here with a reading task.
  • StardustTV does not sell an annual card at all. Every price there is weekly.
How we measured this

Every figure on this page comes from one hands-on session per app, not from a review site. The setup was identical throughout:

  • Environment — Android in BlueStacks, real Google Play billing, signed into a US Google Play account so prices rendered in dollars. No VPN region-switching during the pricing pass.
  • Deriving the per-episode price — every app prices locked episodes in coins, not dollars. We read the coin cost per episode from the unlock screen, then divided by the coins-per-dollar of a given pack. That is why the same episode shows two prices on our tables: $0.50 is the entry pack, and the lower figure is what the largest pack works out to. Both are real, and which one applies depends on the pack you buy.
  • Renewal, not intro — where an app offers a discounted first period, we report the renewal price separately. Intro offers in this category carry countdown timers and rotate by day and by account, so they are treated as marketing, not as the price.
  • Confirmed against the store sheet — for every subscription we cross-checked the renewal figure against the Google Play subscription sheet, which is the number that actually reaches the card. Where the in-app text disagreed with the sheet, we report the sheet.

Is it free? The short answer

Free to download, free to watch for the first ten episodes, then not free.

StardustTV is one of the more honest free tiers in this category, and one of the shorter ones. In our October 2026 testing a 50-episode series unlocked episodes 1 through 10 and locked from episode 11 — a 20% free window, which is the most generous opening we measured, and still nowhere near enough to finish anything.

There is a substantial amount of free material behind that paywall, though, and this is genuinely one of the more generous apps on free coins:

  • a 6-day sign-in chain worth 270 coins in total
  • 5 ad-unlocks a day, each unlocking 5 episodes
  • bonus coin tasks for reading news and articles inside the app

The short version: you can watch quite a lot for free if you are willing to sit through advertisements and open the app every day. You cannot finish a series for free.

Where this sits against the category

For free-episode count, StardustTV is the most generous of the six apps we tested (10 free, against 6–8 for GoodShort and ShortMax). For ad-unlocks it is the least generous (5 a day against 10 for ReelShort and ShortMax). It compensates by unlocking 5 episodes per ad instead of 1.

That article-task row is worth pausing on, because it tells you something about the business model. StardustTV is not only a drama app — it runs a news-and-articles product with its own task economy, and the drama app pays you in that currency. It is a different monetisation shape from the pure coin-and-subscription apps in this category, and it is why the sign-in chain is worth 270 coins rather than the 200-odd we saw elsewhere.

Where it stops being free

Episode 11. In the series we tested, the lock starts there and runs to the end.

The paywall states the price plainly: 65 coins per episode. That is the second-highest per-episode price of the six apps we measured, behind only NetShort’s 98 N coins. For reference, ReelShort charges 60 and GoodShort and ShortMax charge 50.

At roughly a cent a coin that puts a single episode at about $0.65, and a 50-episode series at roughly $32.50 in coins if you pay for all of it from the first block onwards.

There are three ways past the lock, all visible on the same screen:

  • 65 coins per episode
  • watch advertisements — up to 5 ad-unlocks a day
  • subscribe to the weekly VIP

Three weekly prices on one screen

This is the part worth warning you about, and it is not unusual in this category — but StardustTV does it more visibly than most.

During one session we saw three different weekly prices at the same time:

And the 40% does not reconcile. If $13.19 is 60% of the original, the original was about $21.99 — not the $19.99 shown as the list price on the same screen.

That does not make the app dishonest. It is the standard rotating-intro pattern across this whole category: a headline price, a smaller discount, a larger discount with a timer, and no single figure that is simply “the price”.

What to actually take away

Write down the renewal price, not the offer price. In our capture the non-discounted weekly was $19.99, so that is the number to budget against. Every one of these apps rotates its intro offer daily — ReelShort showed $1.99 one day and $15.99 the next — so an offer you see today may not exist tomorrow, and the countdown is not a deadline on a real offer.

We have written this up in more detail across the category in why short drama app prices differ, because it is a mechanic and not a quirk.

There is no bonus on the two smallest packs, which is where most people start. At 65 coins an episode, the $5.99 pack gets you about 9 episodes.

Run the arithmetic the other way: to finish the remaining 40 episodes of the series we tested, from episode 11 to 50, at 65 coins each is 2,600 coins. That is roughly $26 in coins, on top of the ten episodes you got free.

The ads math

Each ad-unlock gives you 5 episodes and there are 5 ad-unlocks a day — so 25 episodes a day if you sit through 10 advertisements. The 40-episode remainder is about a day and a half of advertising. That is a real option, and it costs you no money, but nobody describes it as free.

It says its content is AI-generated

The playback screen in StardustTV carries a label reading “Contains AI-generated content”.

Of the six apps we tested, StardustTV was the only one that disclosed this on the player itself. Others may well use AI-generated material without saying so on screen; we can only report what each app states.

Two things this does not tell you:

  • It is not per-title. The label appears on the player, so it is a category-wide statement rather than a claim about the series you are watching.
  • It does not mean the app is bad. Plenty of legitimate drama apps mix AI assistance into post-production. What it does mean is that if AI-generated footage matters to you, this is the one app that is upfront about it.

We would rather name this than let you find it later, because it is the kind of detail that changes whether a subscription is worth it — and no other app in this category volunteers it.

Is it worth paying for?

Worth it if:

  • You want a large free library to sample and you are willing to watch advertisements — 10 free episodes plus 5 ad-unlocks a day is a lot of viewing
  • you would rather not bill through Google Play, since PayPal is supported
  • AI-disclosed content is a deal-breaker you want handled honestly rather than discovered later

Less worth it if:

  • You want to finish series on coins. At 65 coins an episode, about $0.65, it is one of the more expensive per-episode apps we measured.
  • You dislike advertisement-heavy free routes. Five ad-unlocks a day each requiring two ads is the tightest cap in the category, even though each unlock is worth more.
  • You want a clear subscription price. Three weekly prices coexist on one screen and the 40% discount does not reconcile with the list price.

If you do subscribe: budget against the $19.99/week non-discounted figure, and set the reminder the same day.

Where our testing breaks down

  • We tested one series. The 10-episode free window is what one title did. No app in this category publishes its free-episode policy, and we have seen the boundary vary by series elsewhere in the category. Do not treat 10 as a guarantee.
  • No annual price captured. The payment screen only exposed a Weekly VIP card during our session, so we cannot tell you whether a monthly or annual tier exists — which matters a great deal if you would otherwise be paying $19.99 a week indefinitely.
  • We did not capture a full ad-free tier price. The app advertises ad-free options on some tiers; we cannot quote one.
  • Android only, US Google Play account. Prices elsewhere may differ, and this app supports PayPal, which can have its own regional behaviour.
  • We earn a commission if you subscribe. That is why the cons sections are specific about the 65-coin price and the three-price confusion rather than glossing them — a review that only lists advantages is an advertisement.

Bottom line

StardustTV is free in the sense that most of this category is free: a generous opening window, a decent sign-in chain and ad-unlocks that add up, followed by a paywall at episode 11 of the series we tested.

It is worth choosing if the AI-content disclosure matters to you or you want to pay outside Google Play. It is worth being careful with if you intend to pay by coins — 65 an episode is near the top of the range — or if you are going to subscribe without noting that the non-discounted weekly is $19.99.

Frequently asked questions

Is StardustTV really free?

Free to download and free to watch for the first 10 episodes — the series we tested unlocked episodes 1 through 10 and locked from 11. After that it charges 65 coins per episode, roughly $0.65 at the smallest coin pack. There is no free tier that carries a whole series.

How much does StardustTV cost per episode?

65 coins per episode, stated on the paywall itself. On the smallest pack ($5.99 for 600 coins, about one cent a coin) that works out to $0.65 an episode. On larger packs it drops to about $0.57. That makes it the second most expensive per-episode app we measured, after NetShort.

How many free episodes does StardustTV give?

Ten in the series we tested, out of 50 episodes. There is no published free-episode policy, and we only checked one title, so treat 10 as an example rather than a guarantee. The boundary may vary by series, as it does on the other apps in this category.

Can I watch without ads?

The weekly VIP is a subscription, and the app has multiple tiers including an ad-free option on some of them. We did not capture a full ad-free tier price, so we cannot quote one. What we can confirm is that the free route is ad-supported: 5 ad-unlocks a day, and each unlock requires watching 2 advertisements.

Does StardustTV accept PayPal?

Yes. The payment screen during our October 2026 testing showed PayPal as an option alongside the usual in-app subscription flow. That is uncommon among the short-drama apps we tested and means you are not forced to bill through Google Play.

Is StardustTV content AI-generated?

Partly, and it says so. The playback screen carries a "Contains AI-generated content" label, which made it the only one of the six apps we tested to disclose this on the player itself. Whether a given series is AI-made, live-action, or both is not stated per title, so treat the label as a category-wide warning rather than a per-series fact.
