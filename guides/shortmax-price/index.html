What we ran into while testing

  • The store page printed two per-day figures for the weekly pass at the same time — $2.86 a day and $2.28 a day — with no label on either. We captured the screen because we thought we had misread it.
  • The intro offer ran on a live countdown and reverted to $19.99 a week after two weeks. The cheaper number is real but it expires.
  • The reward ads were full-screen third-party install ads — ours routed to Temu — and they interrupted the flow twice during capture. Ten ad unlocks a day is generous only if you can sit through them.
  • New accounts get a 100-coin welcome bonus, which is a fraction of one episode's 50-coin cost but a real start.
  • Everything above was captured in an emulator with the real Google Play billing SDK, so the dollar prices are real, not estimated.

The two passes

ShortMax keeps its pricing simple on the surface — two passes, no tiers:

PassPriceWhat it works out to
Weekly Pass Pro$19.99 a weekabout $2.86 a day, auto-renewing
Annual Pass Pro$149.99 a yearabout $0.41 a day, auto-renewing

Every price on this page was read inside the app on an Android emulator running the real Google Play billing SDK, on 9 October 2026, in US dollars.

One number does most of the work: $149.99 is about $0.41 a day, while the weekly pass is about $2.86 a day. The annual pass is roughly seven times cheaper per day — so it is the better deal by a wide margin if you stay subscribed past roughly two months. Below that, the weekly pass is the smaller commitment.

Two daily figures on one screen

This is the thing worth knowing before you tap anything.

On the store page we captured, the weekly pass carried two different per-day figures at once:

  • $2.86 a day
  • $2.28 a day

Neither was labelled as the “real” one. $19.99 a week is $2.856 a day, so the first figure is just the weekly price divided by seven. The second — $2.28 — does not match the weekly price at all, and we could not find where it came from on the screen.

We cannot reconcile the two figures, and the app does not try to

We are not going to invent a reason the two numbers differ. What we can say is that this is the only app in our comparison that printed two conflicting per-day prices for one pass on one screen. The number that actually reaches your card is the $19.99 a week on the Google Play subscription sheet, not either of the in-app day figures — so read the Play confirmation before you agree.

The intro price behind a countdown

ShortMax also runs a popup with its own countdown: $12.79 a week for the first two weeks, then back to $19.99. The cheaper number is real, but it expires.

This is the same playbook as the rest of the category — ReelShort and DramaBox do the same thing — so the useful habit is to ignore the intro and price the renewal. The renewal is $19.99 a week, every time.

Coins: 50 an episode

If you would rather pay per episode than subscribe, one locked episode costs 50 coins — the paywall states it directly. The four packs, with the arithmetic done:

The coin route only beats a weekly pass if you have exactly one series in mind. A weekly pass unlocks everything for seven days for $19.99; finishing a single 30-episode series with coins costs about 24 paid episodes × $0.50 = $12 — cheaper than a week, but you lose access the moment you stop buying coins. For a daily habit, the pass wins.

The free route is the real edge

ShortMax’s strongest feature is not its price — it is its free route. On the 30-episode series we tested, episodes 1–6 were free, then the paywall arrived (the shortest opening we measured). But the app allows ten ad unlocks a day, matching ReelShort and ahead of every other app we tested.

That combination is unusual: the shortest free opening, attached to the most generous ad allowance. If you watch daily and will sit through the ads — which are full-screen third-party install ads, in our case a Temu promotion — the app becomes usable for a long time without paying.

What this page does not cover

The central limitation of these figures is that they are a dated measurement (9 October 2026), not a fixed rate — ShortMax rotates prices daily, so treat them as a snapshot.

We did not test the television apps ShortMax advertises — only the Android phone build — so any TV behaviour is unverified by us. We also could not confirm whether the two per-day figures on the store page are a bug or a deliberate tactic; we report only what the screen showed. Treat every price here as a dated measurement (9 October 2026), not a fixed rate.

Bottom line

ShortMax costs $19.99 a week or $149.99 a year, with a $12.79 intro behind a countdown. The annual pass is about seven times cheaper per day, so it is the better deal if you stay past two months. Watch for the store page printing two different per-day figures for the weekly pass — the only price that matters is the $19.99 on your Google Play subscription sheet. And if you will watch daily, the ten ad unlocks a day are the real reason to pick it.

Frequently asked questions

How much does ShortMax cost?

Verified on Android with a US Google Play account on 9 October 2026: Weekly Pass Pro is $19.99 a week and Annual Pass Pro is $149.99 a year, about $0.41 a day. A popup offered the first two weeks at $12.79 a week behind a countdown timer, after which it reverts to $19.99. Coin packs run from $4.99 for 500 coins to $24.99 for 3,375 coins including bonus, and a single episode costs 50 coins.

Why does ShortMax show two different per-day prices?

On the store page we captured, the weekly pass was labelled both $2.86 a day and $2.28 a day at the same time, with no explanation of which one applied. We did not see any other app in this comparison print two different daily figures for one pass on one screen. The only figure that reaches your card is the $19.99 a week on the Google Play subscription sheet, so treat the per-day numbers as marketing, not the price.

Is ShortMax cheaper than ReelShort?

Slightly, on both routes. Its yearly pass is $149.99 against ReelShort's $199.99, and its per-episode coin price is 50 coins against 60 — about $0.50 versus $0.60. The trade-off is that ReelShort starts you with more free episodes and a higher production budget.

How many free episodes does ShortMax give?

On the 30-episode series we tested, episodes 1–6 were free, then the paywall arrived — the shortest free opening of the four apps we measured. After that you have three routes: watch ads (up to ten a day, the best allowance we saw), spend 50 coins per episode, or buy a pass.
