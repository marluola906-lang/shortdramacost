StardustTV Cost Per Season: What One Full Series Really Costs

By marluola Published 28 October 2026 Updated 28 October 2026 Prices checked in-app on 2026-10-09
Short answer
  • One StardustTV series is the season. The 50-episode title we tested gave 10 free episodes — the widest opening of the four apps — then 65 coins each.
  • Paying with coins: 40 paid episodes times 65 coins equals 2,600 coins, about $26 (one $19.99 pack plus one $5.99 pack).
  • Free route: 5 episodes a day via ads, at two ads each. Slower than rivals, and there is no annual pass anywhere in the payment flow we saw.
  • StardustTV quotes three weekly prices ($19.99 / $17.99 / $13.19). Trust the checkout screen, which is where the money actually lands.
  • Because there is no annual pass, long-term watching means 52 weekly payments. Plan around that before subscribing.

What we ran into while testing

  • The same account quoted three weekly prices on the same day: $19.99 on the plan card, $17.99 at checkout, and $13.19 behind a 40%-off coupon popup whose base price never appeared.
  • A 40% discount off the listed $19.99 would be about $12, not $13.19 — so the coupon base price is something the app never showed us.
  • The free opening was the widest we measured: 10 episodes on a 50-episode series, against 6 on ShortMax and 7 on GoodShort.
  • The ad route cost two ads per episode and capped at 5 episodes a day, so finishing a series free took roughly eight days of daily effort.

“Cost per season” is the question people actually have about StardustTV, and it is the one nobody answers. The app does not publish a monthly price, does not publish a per-series price, and quotes its weekly pass three different ways. So we measured it: watched the paywall, counted the episodes, and did the arithmetic on a real series.

What “a season” means here

First, a correction that saves you money. Short-drama apps do not sell seasons the way a television show does. One series is the season. The title we opened ran 50 episodes — but there is no “Season 3” waiting to be sold separately.

That matters because the phrase “cost per season” returns almost nothing accurate. The number to plan around is cost per finished series, not cost per month.

Every figure below comes from an Android emulator running the real Google Play billing SDK with a US account, checked in-app on 9 October 2026, with screenshots at each step. The prices rotate — we saw three weekly figures in one session — so treat them as dated measurements, not a rate card.

The per-season formula

Three steps, and you can run them for any series in under a minute:

  1. Find the episode count. It is on the series page. Count the episodes, not the parts.
  2. Subtract the free block. On our series that was 10 episodes before the paywall. The exact number varies by series and is not disclosed in advance, so open the series and scroll to the first locked episode.
  3. Multiply the remainder by 65 coins — the verified per-episode price — then convert to dollars at the pack rate you would actually buy at.

A worked example

This uses the series we actually opened, so the episode count is real rather than illustrative.

A 50-episode series: about $26

The title we measured had 50 episodes and the paywall arrived at episode 11, so 40 episodes needed unlocking. At 65 coins each that is 2,600 coins. Coins are sold in fixed packs, so the total depends on how you assemble it:

Pack combinationCostCoinsCovers the season?
$19.99 + $5.99$25.982,800Yes, with 200 left over
$9.99 x 3$29.973,000Yes, with 400 left over
$29.99 x 1$29.993,450Yes, with 850 left over

The single $29.99 pack is the worst per season and the best per coin. That is the trap in this pricing: the pack that offers the best rate is only the best value if you finish another season.

The cheapest route, in order

If the goal is to pay as little as possible per season, do these in order and stop whenever the series is finished:

  1. Watch the free block. 10 episodes at no cost. No action needed.
  2. Use the ad route. Five episodes a day, at two ads each. Slow, but it is the only free route past the opening, and a 50-episode series is finishable in about eight days of daily effort.
  3. Then buy coins — and buy them from the unlock popup, not the store page. The store page starts at $19.99. The $5.99 pack exists only inside the popup.
What that means in practice

A viewer who runs the free loop for eight days collects 40 advert-unlocked episodes — enough to finish the paid tail of a 50-episode series without spending. The cost is time, and the ads are two per episode rather than one.

When a pass wins per season

StardustTV sells a weekly pass, not a monthly one, and we saw no annual option. Using the rates we recorded:

Compare that to the coin route, which is about $26 per season and does not care how long you take. The honest read:

  • The coupon price is the cheapest per season — $13.19 beats $26 in one week — but it only appears behind a popup with a one-hour countdown, and its base price is never shown.
  • There is no annual pass, so a year of watching means 52 weekly payments at whatever price reached your checkout screen. That is the structural weakness of this app for a regular viewer.

Where this breaks down

This is a limitation of the method, not a hedge:

  • The free block is not published, so you cannot compute the cost before you start watching. Every calculation here needs the first locked episode number, and the only way to get it is to open the series. This does not work as a spreadsheet exercise; it is a two-minute check inside the app.
  • StardustTV quotes its weekly pass three ways. We trust the checkout screen, but a price that moves between the plan card and the coupon popup is a price you should verify on your own device before paying.
  • We found no annual pass. That is what we saw in the payment flow, not a guarantee the app never offers one. If you are committing for a year, check the current offers in-app before assuming weekly is your only route.
  • These are Android prices from a US-route account. We have not measured iOS billing, and regional pricing is plausible.
  • “Cost per season” is not a comparison between apps. Our free-episode comparison covers the free route across apps.

Bottom line

One full StardustTV series costs about $26 in coins, and nothing on the coin route changes that number based on how long you take. The free allowance — 10 free episodes, then five ad-unlocked episodes a day — is among the most generous openings we measured, but the two-ads-per-episode cost makes the free route slow.

The structural catch is the missing annual pass. Per season, coins at $26 and the coupon weekly at $13.19 both look reasonable. For a year of watching, 52 weekly payments at $13–20 each is $700–1,000, against $99.99–149.99 on rivals that do offer an annual pass. If you plan to stay, that gap matters more than the per- season maths.

If you want the number for your specific series, open it, find the first locked episode, subtract it from the total, multiply by 65 coins, and pick the smallest pack combination that covers it. That is more accurate than anything this page — or any price comparison site — can tell you about your own screen. Frequently asked questions

How much does one full season of a StardustTV series cost?

About $26 in coins. The 50-episode series we measured had 10 free episodes, leaving 40 paid at 65 coins each, or 2,600 coins. The cheapest pack combination to cover that is one $19.99 pack (2,200 coins) plus one $5.99 pack (600 coins), about $26. One episode costs 65 coins, verified in-app on Android in October 2026.

Why is StardustTV priced per series rather than per month?

Because there is no monthly price. The app sells coins for per-episode unlocks and a weekly pass. The useful unit of cost is cost per finished series, which is what this page calculates. Anyone quoting a single monthly figure is guessing at your viewing habit.

Is StardustTV cheaper than the other short-drama apps per season?

Per episode it is among the more expensive: 65 coins against ReelShort 60, DramaBox 50 and ShortMax 50. It partly offsets that with the widest free opening we measured (10 episodes), but finishing a whole series in coins costs more here than on DramaBox, and there is no annual pass to lower the long-term rate.

Can I watch a StardustTV series for free?

The series we tested gave 10 free episodes, then the ad route unlocked 5 episodes a day at two ads each. That is slower than ReelShort or ShortMax (one ad per episode) and there is no published annual pass, so the free route is real but thin.

Does StardustTV have an annual pass?

We did not find one. The payment flow we tested showed only the weekly VIP pass, quoted three different ways ($19.99, $17.99 and $13.19). A plan we did not open is not the same as a plan that does not exist, so read that as what we saw, not a guarantee. But for a year, the only route we found is 52 weekly payments.
