ReelShort vs ShortMax: Which Is Cheaper per Episode?

By marluola Published 18 October 2026 Updated 18 October 2026 Prices checked in-app on 2026-10-09
Short answer
  • Per episode, ShortMax is cheaper: 50 coins against ReelShort's 60 — about $0.37 vs $0.40 at best pack rates. Ten cents apart on the smallest pack.
  • Per year, ShortMax is also cheaper: $149.99 against ReelShort's $199.99. That is $50 a year, and it reverses the usual pattern in this category.
  • Per week it goes the other way: ReelShort renews at $5.99 or $11.99 against ShortMax's flat $19.99. Short one series a month and the weekly gap is what you feel.
  • Finish one 50-episode series on coins: roughly $25 on ShortMax and $30 on ReelShort.
  • ReelShort's free side is far deeper — daily sign-in worth 200 coins, watch-time tasks worth 340 more, and 10–16 free episodes against ShortMax's 6.
  • ReelShort also has native English originals with real casts. ShortMax is mostly dubbed. That is the strongest reason to pick it.

What we ran into while testing

  • On ShortMax a 30-episode drama was free to episode 6 and locked from episode 7 — the tightest free window of the four we measured in the same week.
  • The ShortMax paywall offers all three routes on one screen: watch an ad, spend 50 coins, or subscribe.
  • ShortMax shows two daily figures at once, $2.86 per day and $2.28 per day. We could not work out which one it wanted us to believe.
  • ReelShort scores 4.7 on the App Store and 3.8 on Google Play. The same app scores 1.3 on Trustpilot, where 265 reviews complain almost entirely about billing and cancellation.
How we measured this

Every figure on this page comes from one hands-on session per app, not from a review site. The setup was identical throughout:

  • Environment — Android in BlueStacks, real Google Play billing, signed into a US Google Play account so prices rendered in dollars. No VPN region-switching during the pricing pass.
  • Deriving the per-episode price — every app prices locked episodes in coins, not dollars. We read the coin cost per episode from the unlock screen, then divided by the coins-per-dollar of a given pack. That is why the same episode shows two prices on our tables: $0.50 is the entry pack, and the lower figure is what the largest pack works out to. Both are real, and which one applies depends on the pack you buy.
  • Renewal, not intro — where an app offers a discounted first period, we report the renewal price separately. Intro offers in this category carry countdown timers and rotate by day and by account, so they are treated as marketing, not as the price.
  • Confirmed against the store sheet — for every subscription we cross-checked the renewal figure against the Google Play subscription sheet, which is the number that actually reaches the card. Where the in-app text disagreed with the sheet, we report the sheet.

Known limits of this method — three things we could not control, stated so you can weigh the numbers: introductory offers rotate daily; DramaBox prices by IP region rather than by account, so a different connection can quote a different currency; and neither app publishes its free/paid boundary, so that figure varies by series and is a measured sample rather than a rule.

The 30-second answer

ShortMax is cheaper on both numbers we can verify, and it is not close on either.

Per episode: ShortMax charges 50 coins, ReelShort charges 60. At the pack rates we saw that is $0.37 against $0.40.

Per year: ShortMax sells an Annual Pass Pro at $149.99. ReelShort sells a Yearly VIP at $199.99. ShortMax is $50 a year cheaper.

That second figure is unusual. In this category the established pattern is that the app with the better per-episode price has the worse long-term option — ReelShort versus DramaBox is exactly that shape. ShortMax does not follow it.

Where ReelShort wins: weekly billing (it renews at $5.99 or $11.99 against ShortMax’s flat $19.99) and the free side, which is much deeper — more free episodes, a richer daily coin economy, and native English originals with real casts.

Per episode: ten coins apart

Both apps run the same coin economy, which is why the per-episode prices look similar. You buy coins, and each locked episode costs a fixed number of them.

ReelShort charges 60 coins per episode. ShortMax charges 50. Since coin packs get cheaper per coin the more you buy, the two apps have to be compared at matched pack sizes.

On the smallest pack ($4.99 for 500 coins, about a cent a coin):

  • ReelShort: 60 coins = $0.60
  • ShortMax: 50 coins = $0.50

On the larger packs, where the per-coin rate improves:

  • ReelShort: 5,000 + 2,500 bonus = 7,500 coins for $49.99, so 60 coins ≈ $0.40
  • ShortMax: 2,500 + 875 bonus = 3,375 coins for $24.99, so 50 coins ≈ $0.37

So the honest gap is ten cents on entry and three cents at the best rate. We use the best rate as the main figure because that is what most people end up doing, but it is an assumption about behaviour rather than a fact about the apps.

The knock-on effect is what people actually feel. A 50-episode series costs roughly $25 in ShortMax coins and $30 in ReelShort coins. Both are more than most people expect to pay when they tap “unlock”.

Where the free route changes the maths

Both apps give you 10 ad unlocks a day. If you watch one episode a day, advertisements alone cover it and you pay nothing. The coin arithmetic above only starts mattering past about 10 episodes a day — which is why our free coins guide is worth reading before you buy anything.

ShortMax’s $19.99 weekly looks expensive next to ReelShort’s $5.99 tier — which is the number that dominates search results and marketing pages. But a weekly tier is not a budget; it is an option to pay a lot. The annual tiers are the real comparison, and there ShortMax is $50 ahead.

Worked out over a year, assuming you stay subscribed the whole time:

  • ReelShort Yearly VIP: $199.99
  • ShortMax Annual Pass Pro: $149.99

For someone who watches regularly, that $50 outweighs the three-cent per-episode gap several times over. And for someone who watches very regularly, the weekly tier is the wrong comparison on both apps — you should be on an annual plan.

Both apps rotate their intro offers daily

ReelShort showed a $1.99 first week on 8 October and a $15.99 first week on 9 October, with different discount labels each time and a countdown running. ShortMax’s intro was $12.79/week for the first two weeks with a 39-minute timer on the popup. Write down the renewal price, not the offer price — in our capture the non-discounted weekly on both apps was the reliable figure.

Where ReelShort is much deeper

The price comparison goes to ShortMax. The free comparison goes to ReelShort, and not narrowly.

ReelShort’s free economy (verified 8–9 October 2026):

  • 10–16 free episodes depending on the series — one 81-episode title ran 15 free episodes before episode 16 was paid
  • 10 ad-unlocks a day
  • daily sign-in reaching 200 coins on day 7, with a smaller amount on each of the six days before it
  • watch-time tasks worth up to 340 coins a day (watch 2, 5, 10, 20 or 30 minutes)
  • 17 ad-task coins a day
  • free coupons for new titles in their first 7 days
  • 50-coin first-login gift

ShortMax’s free economy (verified same session):

  • 6 free episodes in the 30-episode series we opened
  • 10 ad-unlocks a day, the same cap
  • 100 bonus coins welcome gift
  • ad unlocks that grant a bonus on top of the unlock (“Auto Pay 50 Bonus”)

Put the two side by side. ReelShort can hand you several hundred free coins a day if you are willing to watch a few advertisements and spend twenty minutes actually watching. ShortMax’s free side is essentially one welcome bonus and the ad-unlock counter.

That difference matters most exactly where the price gap does not: if you watch about one series a month, ReelShort’s free economy probably covers you entirely and the per-episode gap never comes up.

How to decide

  1. Finish roughly one series a month? Start on ReelShort’s free tier and buy coins only if you get stuck. Its daily sign-in and watch-time tasks cover a lot of episodes at no cost, and you are never auto-renewing anything.

  2. Two or more series a month? ShortMax on the $149.99 annual pass. Ten cents an episode and $50 a year cheaper is the better shape for that habit.

  3. Watch on a television? ShortMax is the one of the two listed with TV apps. We have not tested it, so we can confirm the listing rather than the experience.

  4. Care about English-language originals with real casts? ReelShort. This is a taste judgement and it is the strongest reason most viewers pick it — there is no dubbing mismatch to get wrong.

  5. If you subscribe to either: budget the renewal price and set the reminder on the day you sign up. Our cancellation walkthrough covers the mechanics, and the billing route differs between them — which account you actually need to cancel from is the step people get wrong.

Where this comparison breaks down

  • We only tested Android, on a US Google Play account. ReelShort’s tiers above were captured the same way, so the Android comparison is apples to apples. iOS pricing and billing differ, and ReelShort in particular handles subscriptions differently per store.
  • Both apps rotate their introductory prices daily. ReelShort showed $1.99 one day and $15.99 the next with different discount labels. ShortMax’s $12.79 intro also expires. Treat the renewal figures as reliable and the offer figures as a snapshot of 8–9 October 2026.
  • The three-cent per-episode gap assumes you buy the largest pack. Buying only the $4.99 pack widens the gap to ten cents. We used best rates because it matches what most people end up doing, but that is a behavioural assumption.
  • ShortMax’s free-episode count is one title. Six free episodes out of 30 in the series we opened. No app in this category publishes its policy, and we have seen the boundary vary by series elsewhere.
  • We did not test either TV app. The television row above is based on app listings, not on use.
  • We earn a commission on both apps. That is why the annual-pass section recommends ShortMax even though ReelShort is the more popular app — a comparison that always lands on the same recommendation is an advertisement, not a comparison.

Bottom line

ShortMax is cheaper per episode by ten coins and cheaper per year by $50. ReelShort is much deeper on the free side, cheaper if you only subscribe week by week, and it has the better original English content.

Watch one series a month and use ReelShort’s free economy first. Watch regularly and ShortMax’s $149.99 annual pass is the better buy.

Frequently asked questions

Is ShortMax cheaper than ReelShort?

Yes, on both the measures we verified. ShortMax charges 50 coins per episode against ReelShort's 60, so about $0.37 against $0.40 at best pack rates. And ShortMax sells a $149.99 annual pass against ReelShort's $199.99 — $50 a year cheaper. The one place ReelShort wins is weekly billing, where it renews at $5.99 or $11.99 against ShortMax's flat $19.99.

Which is cheaper for finishing a whole series?

ShortMax, by about $5. At 50 coins an episode and roughly $0.0074–$0.0100 a coin, a 50-episode ShortMax series runs about $25. The same series on ReelShort at 60 coins runs about $30. Neither figure is small — both are real money for the same story.

Does ShortMax have an annual subscription?

Yes. ShortMax sells an Annual Pass Pro at $149.99 alongside its Weekly Pass Pro at $19.99. We initially misread this app as having no long-term option, which is worth stating plainly because it was wrong — the annual tier is the single reason ShortMax comes out ahead on annual cost.

Which has more free content?

ReelShort, substantially. It gives 10–16 free episodes depending on the series, a daily sign-in chain reaching 200 coins on day 7, watch-time tasks worth up to 340 coins a day, and 17 ad-task coins. ShortMax gives 6 free episodes, a 100-coin welcome bonus and a 10-per-day ad-unlock cap.

Can I watch ShortMax on a TV?

ShortMax is listed with TV apps, which is a genuine difference from ReelShort, where we could not find a dedicated television build. We have not tested the TV app itself, so treat this as a feature difference rather than a quality verdict.

Which has better content?

ReelShort. It commissions English-language originals shot with real casts, so there is no dubbing to go wrong. ShortMax leans on dubbed and subtitled titles. This is a taste judgement rather than something measurable, and it is the reason most viewers end up on ReelShort regardless of price.
