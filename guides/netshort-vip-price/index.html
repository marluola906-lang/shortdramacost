NetShort VIP Price: Five Weekly Prices on One Screen, and the $99.99 Year

By marluola Published 20 October 2026 Updated 20 October 2026 Prices checked in-app on 2026-10-09
Short answer

  • The weekly pass is where it gets confusing. On one day in October 2026 we saw $7.99, $14.39, $14.99, $15.99 and $24.99 quoted for weekly access in the same app.
  • The number that actually bills you is $24.99 a week. Google Play states it plainly on the subscription sheet: "Starting Oct 16, 2026 $24.99/week + tax". $15.99 is an intro price, and $7.99 is a new-user popup.

What we ran into while testing

  • One NetShort week quoted us $7.99, $14.39, $14.99, $15.99 and $24.99 — five numbers, same app, same day. The screens that showed each one were not the same screen.
  • The $24.99 one is the only price that bills. The Google Play subscription sheet states it in writing: "Starting Oct 16, 2026 $24.99/week + tax". Nothing in the app says that.
  • A competitor site lists NetShort at $30 a week. We did not see that price anywhere — the app and the Play sheet both say $24.99.
  • The coin pack page shows the two subscription tiers and nothing else. There is no coin pack table in the app at all, so the price per coin cannot be worked out before you buy.
How we measured this

Every figure on this page comes from one hands-on session per app, not from a review site. The setup was identical throughout:

  • Environment — Android in BlueStacks, real Google Play billing, signed into a US Google Play account so prices rendered in dollars. No VPN region-switching during the pricing pass.
  • Deriving the per-episode price — every app prices locked episodes in coins, not dollars. We read the coin cost per episode from the unlock screen, then divided by the coins-per-dollar of a given pack. That is why the same episode shows two prices on our tables: $0.50 is the entry pack, and the lower figure is what the largest pack works out to. Both are real, and which one applies depends on the pack you buy.
  • Renewal, not intro — where an app offers a discounted first period, we report the renewal price separately. Intro offers in this category carry countdown timers and rotate by day and by account, so they are treated as marketing, not as the price.
  • Confirmed against the store sheet — for every subscription we cross-checked the renewal figure against the Google Play subscription sheet, which is the number that actually reaches the card. Where the in-app text disagreed with the sheet, we report the sheet.

Known limits of this method — three things we could not control, stated so you can weigh the numbers: introductory offers rotate daily; DramaBox prices by IP region rather than by account, so a different connection can quote a different currency; and neither app publishes its free/paid boundary, so that figure varies by series and is a measured sample rather than a rule.

The two prices that matter

One price on the subscription sheet, and one number on the annual pass. Everything else in the app is noise.

NetShort is the only app in this comparison that shows you its real renewal price before you agree to it. The Google Play subscription sheet reads, in plain text: “Starting Oct 16, 2026 $24.99/week + tax”. That is the number that will bill you, and it is stated before the purchase rather than buried in a settings page afterwards. Credit where it is due.

The two figures worth planning against:

$15.99 is the number most screens advertise. $24.99 is the number you pay. If you budgeted around the promo price, a single renewal will cost you more than you planned for a month of watching.

Five weekly prices on one screen

We recorded five different weekly prices in NetShort on a single day in October 2026. All of them were real screens in the app, not screenshots from other sites.

  • $15.99 on the top-up page, with $24.99 crossed out
  • $7.99 on a new-user popup, with $14.99 crossed out
  • $14.39 on the checkout button — which is $15.99 minus 10%, a Google Pay promotional rate
  • $14.99 and $24.99 as the two ends of the same new-user offer

That is five numbers for one product, on one day, in one app. It is the most extreme version of the price fog we found in this category — StardustTV had three prices coexisting, NetShort had five.

The reason it matters is not that it is confusing. It is that the price you see depends on the screen that happens to load, and you cannot tell in advance which one you will get. The only reliable way to know the renewal price is to read the Google Play subscription sheet, because that is the one place the app cannot decorate.

Five different weekly figures on five different screens is also what the NetShort test log is built around — every screen we opened on 9 October 2026, in the order we opened them.

This is the same mechanism we described in why short drama app prices disagree with every other published price: introductory rates, app-store list prices and web checkout prices are three different numbers for the same plan, and only one of them is what you pay.

The $99.99 year

$99.99 for 365 days of access is the cheapest annual price we found anywhere in this comparison.

That works out to about $0.27 a day, against $24.99 a week for the weekly pass. Anyone who finishes a series in a fortnight should not take the annual pass; this is a plan for people who will watch for months.

Two things to be honest about:

  • It is auto-renewal. If you forget to cancel, it renews at $99.99.
  • We verified the price on a Google Play subscription sheet, so the store will remind you before it charges. That is a genuine advantage over a web checkout.

What one episode costs

98 N coins per episode — the steepest per-episode price of the six apps we tested.

For comparison: DramaBox charges 50 coins a locked episode, ReelShort 60, GoodShort and ShortMax are in the same range, and StardustTV 65. NetShort’s per-episode cost is roughly double the cheapest one we found.

The awkward part is that you cannot price this route before you commit. The top-up page lists the two subscription passes and nothing else — the coin packs are not shown. So the coin route cannot be planned in advance, which is a real disadvantage against apps that publish their pack prices openly.

One more detail worth knowing: a new account starts with 55 N coins, and 98 coins for one episode means the welcome balance is not even enough to unlock a single episode.

Why the annual pass is the only sensible route here

If you are going to use NetShort at all, the coin route is the worst of both worlds — the most expensive per-episode price, and the only one you cannot price up front. Between $99.99 a year and $24.99 a week, the decision is simply how long you will watch.

The free route

Two advertisements per episode. Everyone else in this category needs one.

ReelShort and DramaBox unlock an episode for a single ad. NetShort’s unlock prompt reads “Watch 2 ads to unlock 1 ep”, with a counter going 0/2 to 1/2. It also plays a three-minute video ad, the longest forced advertisement we captured in any of these apps.

The free opening is nine episodes on the 57-episode series we tested — episodes 1 to 9 were free and episode 10 onwards was locked. We only checked one title, so treat nine as an example rather than a policy.

There is a decent sign-in chain behind that: 355 N over seven days (55 on day one, 35 on days two to five, 55 on days six and seven). The catch is that the bonus expires in seven days, and the sign-in button itself is ad-gated — you watch an advertisement to claim the 55 coins. The chain also costs you a tap-through to an offer wall, which is a lower-quality ad environment than the apps that keep you inside their own app.

Is NetShort worth paying for?

It depends entirely on which of the two prices you are looking at.

Take the annual pass if you will watch for months — $0.27 a day is the best number in this entire comparison, and the Play billing sheet means you will be warned before it renews.

Skip the weekly pass entirely. $24.99 a week is $1,299 a year, and for that money DramaBox at $9.99 a week covers as many series as you can watch, with a coin cost per episode that is half of NetShort’s.

What we would not do is pay per episode at 98 N coins, with the pack prices hidden and two ads required per unlock. That is the most expensive way to watch short drama that we measured, and it is the default the app steers you toward when you tap “unlock”.

Where our testing breaks down

We tested NetShort on Android through Google Play in October 2026, and the figures above come from our own screenshots on that date. The limits of that testing are worth stating plainly:

  • We did not see coin pack prices. The top-up page only listed the two subscription passes, so the per-episode cost of 98 coins cannot be turned into a dollar figure. Any claim that NetShort costs a particular amount per episode in dollars is a calculation, not a measured price.
  • We checked one series for the free boundary (57 episodes, 9 free). Other titles may open differently.
  • The daily ad-unlock cap was not visible in our screenshots, so we cannot say how many episodes the free route can realistically cover per day.
  • We did not test the TV build. The app is listed for iOS and Android; we did not verify either a television app or the viewing experience on a large screen.
  • Prices outside the United States were not tested. All figures are the US Google Play store, in US dollars, before tax.

Frequently asked questions

How much is NetShort VIP?

Two figures matter. The annual pass is $99.99 for 365 days, about $0.27 a day. The weekly pass bills $24.99 a week after the intro price, and Google Play states that directly on the subscription sheet: "Starting Oct 16, 2026 $24.99/week + tax". The $15.99 figure you may see advertised is the first week only.

What is the cheapest NetShort subscription?

The $99.99 annual pass, at about $0.27 a day. It was the cheapest annual price we recorded across all six apps we tested. But if you only want to finish one series, an annual pass is more commitment than you need — a week of DramaBox membership at $9.99 covers as many series as you can watch.

Why does NetShort show different prices every time I open it?

We saw five weekly figures in one day: $7.99, $14.39, $14.99, $15.99 and $24.99. The $24.99 is the real renewal price. The $15.99 is the intro rate, $7.99 is a new-user popup, and $14.39 is a Google Pay promotion off $15.99. Which one you see depends on which screen loads and whether the app thinks you are a new user.

How much is one episode on NetShort?

98 N coins. That is the most expensive per-episode price we measured — DramaBox charges 50 and ReelShort 60. We could not price the coin route in advance because coin packs are not shown in the app; the top-up page only lists the two passes.

How many free episodes does NetShort give?

Nine on the series we tested, out of 57. Episodes 1 to 9 were free and episode 10 onwards carried a lock. We only checked one title, so treat nine as an example rather than a published policy.

Is NetShort cheaper than DramaBox?

Not for finishing a series. DramaBox takes $9.99 a week for as many series as you can watch, and one locked DramaBox episode costs 50 coins against NetShort 98. NetShort only wins if you commit to the full year and watch enough to use it.
