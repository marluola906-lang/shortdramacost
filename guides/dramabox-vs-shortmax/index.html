DramaBox vs ShortMax: Same 50 Coins, $7 Apart a Week

By marluola Published 1 November 2026 Updated 1 November 2026 Prices checked in-app on 2026-10-09
Short answer
  • Per episode the two apps are the same product. Both charge 50 coins, and at every coin pack the price lands within one cent of each other: $0.50 / $0.45 / $0.41 / $0.36 on DramaBox against $0.50 / $0.45 / $0.42 / $0.37 on ShortMax.
  • The subscriptions are not close. DramaBox renews at $12.99 a week and $119.99 a year. ShortMax renews at $19.99 a week and $149.99 a year — $7 a week, $30 a year.
  • Over your first four weeks that gap is bigger than it looks: $42.96 on DramaBox against $65.56 on ShortMax, because DramaBox runs its intro for three weeks and ShortMax for two.
  • DramaBox also has a coin pack that lands exactly on one series. Its $12.99 pack holds 1,600 coins = 32 episodes exactly, with nothing left over. ShortMax has no pack that lands on a whole series — you stack two, and unlocking the same 24 episodes costs $12.99 versus $14.98.
  • ShortMax wins exactly one category we measured: 10 ad unlocks a day against DramaBox 8. That is a real difference if you refuse to pay, and it is the only one we found.
  • Its annual pass also breaks even faster — 7.5 weeks of weekly renewals against DramaBox 9.2 — because its weekly price is higher, not because the pass is cheaper.

What we ran into while testing

  • On DramaBox we hit the paywall at episode 12 of a 43-episode drama — 11 free episodes. On ShortMax the lock started at episode 7 of a 30-episode one — 6 free.
  • DramaBox's $12.99 pack holds 1,600 coins, which is exactly 32 episodes at 50 coins each. No leftover at all. That is the only pack in either app that lands on a whole series exactly.
  • ShortMax has no pack that fits its own tested series: 24 locked episodes need 1,200 coins, and the ladder goes from 1,100 straight to 2,400.
  • ShortMax allowed 10 ad unlocks a day against DramaBox's 8. It is the only category where it led in our session.
  • We did not find a daily sign-in chain on ShortMax while testing; the only free-coin entry we logged was a 100-coin welcome bonus. That is a gap in our notes, not proof that none exists.
How we measured this

Every figure on this page comes from one hands-on session per app, not from a review site. The setup was identical throughout:

  • Environment — Android in BlueStacks, real Google Play billing, signed into a US Google Play account so prices rendered in dollars. No VPN region-switching during the pricing pass.
  • Deriving the per-episode price — every app prices locked episodes in coins, not dollars. We read the coin cost per episode from the unlock screen, then divided by the coins-per-dollar of a given pack. That is why the same episode shows two prices on our tables: $0.50 is the entry pack, and the lower figure is what the largest pack works out to. Both are real, and which one applies depends on the pack you buy.
  • Renewal, not intro — where an app offers a discounted first period, we report the renewal price separately. Intro offers in this category carry countdown timers and rotate by day and by account, so they are treated as marketing, not as the price.
  • Confirmed against the store sheet — for every subscription we cross-checked the renewal figure against the Google Play subscription sheet, which is the number that actually reaches the card. Where the in-app text disagreed with the sheet, we report the sheet.

Known limits of this method — three things we could not control, stated so you can weigh the numbers: introductory offers rotate daily; DramaBox prices by IP region rather than by account, so a different connection can quote a different currency; and neither app publishes its free/paid boundary, so that figure varies by series and is a measured sample rather than a rule.

The short answer

If you are choosing between these two apps on the price of an episode, there is nothing to choose. Both charge 50 coins per locked episode, and both sell coins on a four-step ladder that works out to almost the same money: at the entry pack they are identical at $0.50 an episode, and at the top pack they differ by one cent.

The decision is made by the subscription, and there the two apps are not close. DramaBox renews at $12.99 a week and $119.99 a year. ShortMax renews at $19.99 a week and $149.99 a year. Same product — unlimited access to everything — for $7 more a week and $30 more a year.

That is the whole comparison in one line. Everything below is the working behind it, plus the two places where the gap is wider than the headline numbers suggest and the one place ShortMax genuinely wins.

Why we lead with the subscription, not the coins

Coin pricing across this category has converged so hard that it is nearly useless as a tiebreaker. We have now measured five apps at 50 or 60 coins an episode, and the per-coin price at the paid tiers lands on $0.01 almost everywhere — the differences you see between packs are bonus percentages, not cheaper coins. The subscription is the only line on the bill where these apps still disagree.

Read that table again and notice what it does not contain: a reason to switch. Two tiers are identical to the cent, one differs by a cent in DramaBox’s favour, and the entry pack — the one you are shown first — is the same $0.50 on both. If you buy coins, you are buying the same thing.

The interesting difference is at the bottom of the ladder, and it is about size rather than rate. ShortMax starts at 500 coins for $4.99 while DramaBox starts at 300 for $2.99. ShortMax’s cheapest purchase is $2 more, and it gives you ten episodes’ worth instead of six. If you only want to unlock the last stretch of one series, DramaBox lets you buy a smaller block.

The per-episode table is a decoy

Both apps want you to compare $0.50 against $0.50 and conclude the choice does not matter. It matters enormously — just not here. A reader who stops at the coin table will pay $7 a week more than they needed to.

$22.60 apart — ShortMax costs 53% more over the first month. That is a larger gap than the weekly headline suggests, and it exists for a boring reason: DramaBox discounts three weeks, ShortMax discounts two, and DramaBox’s intro price is lower to begin with.

The reverse of that shows up in the annual pass. A year of ShortMax at $149.99 breaks even after about 7.5 weeks of weekly renewals. DramaBox’s $119.99 takes about 9.2 weeks. ShortMax’s annual pass is the better deal relative to its own weekly price — it is just a worse deal in absolute terms.

So which is it — cheap weekly or cheap annual?

Both, for DramaBox: it is cheaper on every line of the subscription table. There is no combination of these plans where ShortMax comes out ahead on price. If you find yourself reading a comparison that says otherwise, check whether it is comparing DramaBox’s annual rate to ShortMax’s weekly one.

The pack that fits one series

Most people are not buying a subscription — they are finishing one drama. That is where the two ladders behave differently, and it has nothing to do with the per-coin rate.

DramaBox: a 43-episode drama, 11 free

That leaves 32 episodes × 50 coins = 1,600 coins. The $12.99 pack holds 1,600. Not “about 1,600” — exactly 1,600, with zero coins left over. Total: $12.99.

ShortMax: a 30-episode drama, 6 free

That leaves 24 episodes × 50 coins = 1,200 coins. ShortMax’s ladder goes 500 → 1,100 → 2,400. There is no 1,200-coin pack. You either buy the 2,400 pack for $19.99 and leave 1,200 coins sitting there, or you stack a $4.99 pack of 500 on top of a $9.99 pack of 1,100 for 1,600 coins at $14.98.

Buying more coins than you need is not free

A pack that overshoots by 1,200 coins looks harmless because “you keep them”. You do not keep them in any useful sense — they sit in an app you may not open again, and they do not carry to another title’s paywall rules. The cheapest route is the smallest pack that still covers the series, and on ShortMax that is two packs, not the big one.

Which one to pick

In the order these factors are likely to decide it for you:

  1. You are subscribing — DramaBox, and it is not close. $12.99 against $19.99 a week, $119.99 against $149.99 a year. ShortMax never wins a subscription line.

  2. You are finishing one series — DramaBox, by $1.99. Its $12.99 pack fits 32 episodes exactly; ShortMax needs two packs to cover 24.

  3. You will not pay at all — ShortMax. Ten ad unlocks a day against eight, plus a 100-coin welcome bonus. If your plan is to never enter card details, this is the one category where ShortMax is the better app.

  4. You want the smaller first purchase — DramaBox. $2.99 for 300 coins against $4.99 for 500. Useful if you only want to unlock the last few episodes of something.

What this comparison cannot tell you

  • One series per app. The free-episode counts come from a 43-episode title on DramaBox and a 30-episode title on ShortMax. Both apps move the free block by title and neither publishes it, so these are samples, not rules.

  • Android only, October 2026. Both were priced through Google Play. We have not verified iOS pricing, which can differ after Apple’s cut is applied.

  • Intro offers rotate. DramaBox’s three-week intro carries a 24-hour countdown, and ShortMax’s two-week intro carries its own timer. What you see on the day you install may not be what we photographed.

  • DramaBox prices by IP country. Same account, same device, different currency depending on where your connection exits. Our figures are the US-dollar ones; the yen prices we captured on the same day were materially different.

  • Gaps in the free-coin tables. We did not capture ShortMax’s sign-in chain or DramaBox’s welcome bonus. Those blanks are ours, not the apps’.

  • Content quality is not assessed here. We are a price comparison. Where the two apps differ on dubbing, originals or catalogue depth, we have not done the testing to rank them.

Bottom line

Per episode they are the same app. Fifty coins each, $0.50 at the entry pack, one cent apart at the top. Nobody should switch apps over this table.

Per subscription they are not. $12.99 against $19.99 a week, $119.99 against $149.99 a year, and $42.96 against $65.56 for the first four weeks. DramaBox is cheaper on every plan it sells.

ShortMax’s case is narrow and specific: more ad unlocks a day, a welcome bonus, and — for the same 24 episodes — a $1.99 higher coin bill because its ladder has no pack that fits. If you are paying, DramaBox. If you are watching adverts and never intend to pay, ShortMax gives you two more episodes a day for it.

Frequently asked questions

Is DramaBox cheaper than ShortMax?

On subscriptions, yes, clearly. DramaBox renews at $12.99 a week and $119.99 a year; ShortMax renews at $19.99 a week and $149.99 a year. That is $7 a week and $30 a year. On coins the two are effectively identical: both charge 50 coins an episode, and the cheapest rate works out at $0.36 on DramaBox against $0.37 on ShortMax.

Do DramaBox and ShortMax charge the same per episode?

Yes — 50 coins each, on both apps. What differs is what a coin costs, which depends on the pack you buy. At the smallest pack both work out at $0.50 an episode. At the largest pack DramaBox is $0.36 and ShortMax $0.37. The coin price is identical, so the episode price is nearly identical too.

How much is the first month on each app?

DramaBox charges $9.99 a week for three weeks then $12.99, so four weeks cost $42.96. ShortMax charges $12.79 a week for two weeks then $19.99, so four weeks cost $65.56. ShortMax costs $22.60 more over that period — 53% more.

Which app gives more free episodes?

DramaBox, in our tests. Its 43-episode drama was free to episode 11 and locked at episode 12. ShortMax gave six free episodes of a 30-episode drama and locked from episode 7. Neither app publishes its free block and both move by title, so treat these as measured samples rather than rules.

Is the ShortMax annual pass worth it?

It breaks even after about 7.5 weeks of weekly renewals, which is faster than DramaBox at about 9.2 weeks — but only because ShortMax charges more per week. Against DramaBox it is still $30 more expensive for the same unlimited access. Buy it if you are staying on ShortMax; do not buy it as a way to save against DramaBox.
