StardustTV Price: $19.99 a Week, Three Weekly Prices, and No Annual Plan

By marluola Published 28 October 2026 Updated 28 October 2026 Prices checked in-app on 2026-10-09
Short answer
  • StardustTV quotes three weekly prices for the same pass: $19.99 on the plan card, $17.99 at checkout with a 10% discount, and $13.19 behind a 40%-off coupon popup with a one-hour countdown.
  • The coupon's base price is never shown. A 40% discount off $19.99 would be about $11.99, not $13.19 — so the "$13.19" does not reconcile with the listed price. Trust the checkout screen: $17.99 is the figure that reaches your card.
  • No annual pass appeared anywhere in the payment flow we tested. We saw only the weekly pass. If you want a year, the only route we found is 52 weeks of paying — and which weekly price you get depends on which popup reaches you first.
  • Coins are 65 per episode — about $0.65 from the $5.99 pack, one of the more expensive per-episode rates we measured. Finishing a 50-episode series runs roughly $26 with coins.
  • The free opening is the widest we found — 10 episodes before the paywall, against 6–7 elsewhere. StardustTV also accepts PayPal at checkout and labels titles that contain AI-generated content.

What we ran into while testing

  • Three weekly prices appeared in one session on one account: $19.99 on the plan card, $17.99 at checkout, and $13.19 behind a coupon popup with a one-hour countdown.
  • The 40%-off coupon's base price was never shown. $13.19 is not 40% off $19.99 (that would be ~$11.99), so the discount claim does not reconcile with the listed price.
  • We scrolled the entire payment flow and never saw an annual pass — only the weekly VIP card. A plan we did not open is not the same as a plan that does not exist, so we report exactly that.
  • The playback screen carried an explicit "Contains AI-generated content" label, which no other app we tested displays. StardustTV also accepted PayPal at checkout.
  • Everything above was captured in an emulator with the real Google Play billing SDK, so the dollar prices are real, not estimated.

Three prices for one pass

On the same day, on the same account, StardustTV quoted us three weekly prices for the same VIP pass:

ScreenWeekly priceNote

Checkout$17.99A 10% discount applied automatically
Coupon popup$13.19Labelled 40% off, with a 59:56 countdown

Every price on this page was read inside the app on an Android emulator running the real Google Play billing SDK, on 9 October 2026, in US dollars.

Our rule when a price is quoted three ways is to trust the checkout screen, because that is the number that reaches your card. But it is worth knowing the cheapest figure only appears if you wait for the right popup.

The coupon that does not add up

The $13.19 coupon is the cheapest weekly figure we recorded anywhere in this comparison — but the math behind it is broken.

A 40% discount off the listed $19.99 would be about $11.99, not $13.19. That implies a base price the app never showed us. We are not going to reconstruct it.

We report the discrepancy, we do not resolve it

The coupon popup prints “40% OFF” and “$13.19/week” but never states the base price it discounts. Because $13.19 is not 40% of $19.99, the claim does not reconcile with the plan card. The only figure we can stand behind is the $17.99 at checkout, which the app actually charged. Treat the $13.19 as “a number a popup showed us,” not a price you can rely on.

No annual plan in the flow

This is the part that sets StardustTV apart from every other app here.

We scrolled the entire payment flow — store page, checkout, coupon popup — and never saw an annual pass. Only the weekly VIP card appeared.

A plan we did not open is not the same as a plan that does not exist, so we will not claim StardustTV has no annual option anywhere. What we can say is: in the payment flow we tested, the only route was 52 weeks of paying, and the per-week price depended on which popup reached us first.

The widest free opening

Where StardustTV genuinely leads: the free opening is the widest we found. On the 50-episode series we tested, episodes 1–10 were free before the paywall at episode 11 — against 6–7 elsewhere.

Its ad route costs more per episode, though: two ads per unlock, five episodes a day. That is the same daily cap as GoodShort but twice the ads per episode, and well behind ReelShort and ShortMax at one ad per episode.

What this page does not cover

The main limitation of this page is that we tested one series in one session, so the figures are a snapshot (9 October 2026), not a guarantee — StardustTV rotates its prices and its free boundary by title and by day.

We tested one series in one session, so the “whole series are free” reputation deserves more evidence than a single test — we could not reproduce it. We also did not capture an annual pass anywhere; it may exist on a screen we did not reach. Treat every price here as a dated measurement (9 October 2026), not a fixed rate.

Bottom line

StardustTV costs $19.99 a week listed, $17.99 at checkout, with a $13.19 coupon that does not reconcile with its own discount. It gives the widest free opening (10 episodes) and accepts PayPal, but shows no annual plan in the payment flow we tested — so a year means 52 weekly payments. Watch the checkout screen, not the popups, for the price that actually reaches your card.

Frequently asked questions

How much does StardustTV cost?

Verified on Android with a US Google Play account on 9 October 2026: the weekly VIP pass is listed at $19.99 a week, drops to $17.99 at checkout with a 10% discount, and a coupon popup offered $13.19 a week at 40% off. Coin packs run from $5.99 for 600 coins to $29.99 for 3,450 coins including bonus. No annual pass appeared in the payment flow we saw. Confirm the figure on your own checkout screen before paying, because the app quotes three different weekly prices.

Is StardustTV really free for whole series?

That is its reputation, but in our testing the series we opened gave us ten free episodes and then a paywall at episode 11, where unlocking cost 65 coins or two ads. We cannot rule out that some titles are fully free, but we could not reproduce the claim on the series we tried, so we report the boundary we actually saw.

Why does StardustTV show $13.19 if it claims 40% off?

A 40% discount off the listed $19.99 would be about $11.99, not $13.19. The coupon popup never shows the base price it discounts, so the math does not reconcile with the plan card. We report the discrepancy rather than guess the missing base. The checkout screen at $17.99 is the figure the app actually charges.

Does StardustTV have an annual plan?

Not in the payment flow we tested. The store and checkout only offered the weekly VIP pass. If you want a year, the only route we found is to pay weekly for 52 weeks, and the price you pay depends on which popup reaches you. Other apps in this comparison (DramaBox, GoodShort, ShortMax, ReelShort) all show an annual tier.
