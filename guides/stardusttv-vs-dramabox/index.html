StardustTV vs DramaBox: Which One Costs Less to Watch

By marluola Published 28 October 2026 Updated 28 October 2026 Prices checked in-app on 2026-10-09
Short answer
  • Per episode DramaBox is cheaper: 50 coins (about $0.50) against StardustTV 65 coins (about $0.65) — the second-priciest single-episode rate we measured.
  • Subscriptions separate them hard. DramaBox is $12.99 a week after three intro weeks and $119.99 a year; StardustTV quotes three weekly prices ($19.99 / $17.99 / $13.19) and no annual pass.
  • On free episodes they are close: StardustTV 10 and DramaBox about 11 on the series we tested, but DramaBox claims 16,000+ titles against StardustTV a mid-sized catalogue.
  • StardustTV takes PayPal at checkout and labels AI-generated titles openly; DramaBox does neither but is the structurally cheaper long-term buy.
  • For finishing one series, DramaBox. For watching free before paying, StardustTV. For a year, only DramaBox offers an annual pass.

What we ran into while testing

  • StardustTV quoted three weekly prices on one account in one session: $19.99 on the plan card, $17.99 at checkout, and $13.19 behind a 40%-off coupon popup whose base price never appeared.
  • A 40% discount off the listed $19.99 would be about $12, not $13.19 — so the coupon base price is something the app never showed us.
  • StardustTV gave 10 free episodes of a 50-episode drama, the widest opening we measured; DramaBox gave about 11 of a 43-episode drama, then 50 coins per episode.
  • Only StardustTV accepted PayPal at checkout, and only StardustTV labelled its AI-generated titles on the playback screen.
How we measured this

Every figure on this page comes from one hands-on session per app, not from a review site. The setup was identical throughout:

  • Environment — Android in BlueStacks, real Google Play billing, signed into a US Google Play account so prices rendered in dollars. No VPN region-switching during the pricing pass.
  • Deriving the per-episode price — every app prices locked episodes in coins, not dollars. We read the coin cost per episode from the unlock screen, then divided by the coins-per-dollar of a given pack. That is why the same episode shows two prices on our tables: $0.50 is the entry pack, and the lower figure is what the largest pack works out to. Both are real, and which one applies depends on the pack you buy.
  • Renewal, not intro — where an app offers a discounted first period, we report the renewal price separately. Intro offers in this category carry countdown timers and rotate by day and by account, so they are treated as marketing, not as the price.
  • Confirmed against the store sheet — for every subscription we cross-checked the renewal figure against the Google Play subscription sheet, which is the number that actually reaches the card. Where the in-app text disagreed with the sheet, we report the sheet.

Known limits of this method — three things we could not control, stated so you can weigh the numbers: introductory offers rotate daily; DramaBox prices by IP region rather than by account, so a different connection can quote a different currency; and neither app publishes its free/paid boundary, so that figure varies by series and is a measured sample rather than a rule.

The short answer

These two apps sit at opposite ends of the category. DramaBox is the volume play — biggest catalogue, lowest cost per finished series, subscription-first. StardustTV is the “try before you pay” play — widest free opening we measured, PayPal at checkout, but a muddier price and no annual pass.

The useful finding is that DramaBox is cheaper on almost every number, while StardustTV wins only on the free opening and payment flexibility. Pick by which of those you actually care about.

One honest caveat before the tables

StardustTV quotes its weekly pass three different ways in the same session ($19.99, $17.99, $13.19), and the coupon’s base price is never shown. We treat the $19.99 list price as the figure that matters, because that is what appears on the plan card. DramaBox’s prices were stable in our test.

That leaves 40 episodes times 65 coins = 2,600 coins. The $19.99 plus $5.99 packs cover it at $25.98 in coins — or free via 5 ad unlocks a day (two ads each) for about eight days.

Which one to pick

In order of how likely each factor is to decide it for you:

  1. You will subscribe — pick DramaBox. $119.99 a year against no annual pass on StardustTV, and $12.99 against $19.99 a week.

  2. You will buy coins to finish one series — pick DramaBox. $12.99 against $25.98, and a shorter paywall to get past.

  3. You want to watch free before paying — consider StardustTV. 10 free episodes is the widest opening we measured, and PayPal is convenient.

  4. You want the biggest catalogue — pick DramaBox. 16,000+ claimed titles against StardustTV’s mid-sized library.

What this comparison cannot tell you

  • StardustTV’s annual pass may exist elsewhere. We only saw the weekly pass in the payment flow. We report what we saw, not a guarantee the app never offers an annual tier.

  • One series per app. Our free-episode counts come from a 43-episode title on DramaBox and a 50-episode title on StardustTV. Neither app publishes its free block and both move by title, so treat these as samples.

  • Android only, October 2026. Both were tested through Google Play on an emulator with real billing. We have not verified iOS pricing.

  • Catalogue depth is not measured. DramaBox claims 16,000+ titles; we did not count StardustTV’s, and content quality is outside our price scope.

Bottom line

Per episode, DramaBox wins. Fifty coins against 65, about $0.50 against $0.65 — a 23% gap that holds at every pack size.

Per subscription, DramaBox wins clearly. $119.99 a year against no annual pass on StardustTV, and $12.99 against $19.99 a week. Same unlimited model, materially different price.

StardustTV’s only real advantages are the free opening (10 episodes) and PayPal. If those are what you came for, it is defensible. Otherwise DramaBox is the cheaper app to watch, to finish a series on, and to subscribe to for a year. Check the renewal price in-app before you commit to either — we verified DramaBox’s, and StardustTV’s three weekly prices mean you should read your own checkout screen.

Frequently asked questions

Is StardustTV cheaper than DramaBox?

No. StardustTV charges 65 coins per episode (about $0.65) against DramaBox 50 coins (about $0.50), and its weekly pass lists at $19.99 against DramaBox $12.99 after the intro. StardustTV does give 10 free episodes against DramaBox about 11, and it accepts PayPal, but on the numbers DramaBox is the cheaper app to watch and to subscribe to. StardustTV's only cheaper moment is its $13.19 coupon week, which is a popup, not the list price.

How much is one episode on StardustTV vs DramaBox?

StardustTV is 65 coins, DramaBox 50. At the smallest pack both work out near $0.50/$0.65 an episode; the gap holds across every pack because the coin count, not the pack, sets the per-episode cost. DramaBox is about 23% cheaper per episode.

Does StardustTV have an annual pass like DramaBox?

We did not find one. The payment flow we tested showed only the weekly VIP pass, quoted three different ways ($19.99, $17.99 and $13.19). A plan we did not open is not the same as a plan that does not exist, so read that as what we saw. DramaBox does offer an annual pass at $119.99, which is the structurally cheaper route for a year of watching.

Which has more free episodes?

Close. StardustTV gave 10 free episodes of a 50-episode series; DramaBox gave about 11 of a 43-episode series. Neither publishes its free block and both vary by title, so treat these as measured samples. The bigger difference is catalogue size: DramaBox claims 16,000+ titles, StardustTV a mid-sized library.

Is StardustTV worth it over DramaBox?

Only for two narrow reasons: you want the widest free opening before the paywall, and you want to pay by PayPal. On price per episode, price per week, and annual value, DramaBox wins. Pick StardustTV if the free episodes are enough to decide, otherwise DramaBox is the better buy.
