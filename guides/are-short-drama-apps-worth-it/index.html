The Best Short Drama Apps: Six We Paid For, Ranked by What You Actually Get

By marluola Published 25 October 2026 Updated 25 October 2026 Prices checked in-app on 2026-10-09
Short answer
  • There is no single best short drama app. The right one depends on how much you watch and how long you stay. DramaBox is the cheapest per episode. NetShort has the cheapest annual pass by a wide margin. StardustTV is the most generous before it starts charging — though the free window moves with the title, and ReelShort gave more on the longer series we tested.
  • If you watch one series and stop, buy the smallest thing that unlocks the episodes you need. A week of DramaBox costs $12.99. A week of NetShort costs $24.99 — nearly double, for one episode at 98 coins.
  • If you will watch for months, the annual pass changes everything. NetShort at $99.99 a year comes to about 27 cents a day. ReelShort at $199.99 comes to about 55 cents a day, twice as much for the same year of access.
  • ShortMax and GoodShort both cost $19.99 a week and are the hardest to tell apart on price. The difference shows up in the free allowance: GoodShort unlocks with one ad, ShortMax offers the same three routes on one screen.
  • Every figure here came from one paid account per app on Android in October 2026. Two of the six quote a different price in their own web checkout than in the app, so check the screen you are actually paying on.

What we ran into while testing

  • Ranked by weekly renewal, the six apps ran from $12.99 to $24.99. Ranked by annual pass, the order is almost reversed: NetShort $99.99 against ReelShort $199.99.
  • The same app can be the cheapest and the most expensive depending on which number you look at. That is not a contradiction, it is a choice about which one the app is steering you towards.
  • StardustTV gave 10 free episodes of a 50-episode drama and locked from episode 11 — the most generous free window we measured.
  • Every one of these subscriptions is sold through Google Play, so every one of them has to be cancelled in the store. None of the apps offers an in-app cancel.
How we measured this

Every figure on this page comes from one hands-on session per app, not from a review site. The setup was identical throughout:

  • Environment — Android in BlueStacks, real Google Play billing, signed into a US Google Play account so prices rendered in dollars. No VPN region-switching during the pricing pass.
  • Deriving the per-episode price — every app prices locked episodes in coins, not dollars. We read the coin cost per episode from the unlock screen, then divided by the coins-per-dollar of a given pack. That is why the same episode shows two prices on our tables: $0.50 is the entry pack, and the lower figure is what the largest pack works out to. Both are real, and which one applies depends on the pack you buy.
  • Renewal, not intro — where an app offers a discounted first period, we report the renewal price separately. Intro offers in this category carry countdown timers and rotate by day and by account, so they are treated as marketing, not as the price.
  • Confirmed against the store sheet — for every subscription we cross-checked the renewal figure against the Google Play subscription sheet, which is the number that actually reaches the card. Where the in-app text disagreed with the sheet, we report the sheet.

Known limits of this method — three things we could not control, stated so you can weigh the numbers: introductory offers rotate daily; DramaBox prices by IP region rather than by account, so a different connection can quote a different currency; and neither app publishes its free/paid boundary, so that figure varies by series and is a measured sample rather than a rule.

The short answer

There is no best short drama app, because the six we tested are good at different things and the right choice depends on how you actually watch.

What we found is that the ranking changes completely depending on which number you look at. By weekly renewal, NetShort is the most expensive app on the list. By annual pass, it is the cheapest by a factor of two. By what one episode costs, DramaBox wins. By how much you get before you pay, ReelShort and StardustTV win.

So the honest answer is a set of answers:

  • One series, then you stop: DramaBox. $12.99 a week, and $0.50 an episode.
  • You will watch for months: NetShort. $99.99 a year is about 27 cents a day, and nothing else comes close.
  • You want to watch as much as possible without paying: ReelShort, which gives away the most and unlocks ten episodes a day with ads.
  • You are not sure and want to start small: DramaBox, because its first three weeks are $9.99 and its smallest coin pack is $2.99.
We are not ranking these as a recommendation

Every app here takes a commission on what you spend, so we have no reason to push one over another. What follows is a comparison of measured prices, with the method and the date on every figure.

Which app suits how much you watch

The single most useful thing we can tell you is that there are two separate questions here, and most comparisons only answer the first one.

The first is what it costs to get into an episode. The second is what it costs to keep watching. Those two questions have almost opposite answers across the six apps, and mixing them up is how people end up paying four times what they needed to.

If you are a casual viewer

You watch a series, you finish it, you move on. Buying a membership for that is a waste. What you want is the cheapest path to the episodes you have not seen.

DramaBox is the cheapest here on both counts. Its weekly membership starts at $9.99 for the first three weeks and renews at $12.99, and one episode costs 50 coins — about $0.50 at the smallest pack size. Its smallest coin pack is $2.99 for 300 coins, which is the lowest commitment we found on any of the six.

NetShort is the opposite. Its first week is $15.99 and the renewal is $24.99. One episode costs 98 N coins, the most expensive unlock of the six, and the new-account balance of 55 coins could not cover even one episode.

If you are a binge watcher

If you are going to be in these apps for months, the weekly price stops being the thing that matters, because you are buying the same access over and over.

At that point the annual pass is the only number worth reading, and it tells a completely different story. We come back to this below.

What one episode actually costs

The per-episode number is the one that is hardest to get right from a price list, because it depends on how much you buy.

One number here is a trap. The surface reading is that 50 coins is cheaper than 60 coins, and that NetShort at 98 is absurd. But the unit price depends on the pack, not the episode.

On ReelShort, a $4.99 pack of 500 coins works out at about $0.0100 a coin, while the $49.99 pack of 7,500 coins works out at about $0.0067. So the 60-coin episode costs $0.60 on a small pack and about $0.40 on a large one. The pack size moves the per-episode price more than the app does.

NetShort is the one case we cannot price this way, and we think it is worth saying why: the top-up page shows the two subscription tiers and no coin packs at all. There is no in-app price per coin to calculate from, so the 98-coin episode has no comparable number. If you are on NetShort, the subscription is the only route we can price.

Buy the biggest pack you will actually finish

The same coins cost 50% more per coin in the small pack than in the large one on ReelShort. A $4.99 pack looks cheap because the number is small. It is the worst value in the app.

When the annual pass is the better buy

This is where the ranking flips, and it is the part most “best app” lists leave out.

Measured by weekly renewal, NetShort is the most expensive app we tested at $24.99. Measured by annual pass, it is the cheapest by a factor of two.

ReelShort looks like the worst yearly buy on the weekly comparison — $18.99 a week against NetShort’s $24.99 — yet over a full year it costs twice as much. The weekly and annual prices are not two versions of the same fact; they are steering you towards different conclusions.

StardustTV is the exception worth planning around. No annual card appeared in the payment flow we saw, and every price that did appear was weekly — so a year of StardustTV looked like 52 weeks of it. If you plan to watch for a year, it is worth checking the app yourself before assuming 52 weeks is the only way in.

ReelShort’s row is the interesting one. On an 81-episode drama it was free to episode 14 and paid from 16. On a 54-episode drama it was free to around episode 10. The same app, the same week, a different free window depending on the show — which is why we will not write a single number for it anywhere on this site.

On top of the opening, ad unlocks extend the free run:

  • ReelShort and ShortMax: 10 ad-unlocks a day
  • DramaBox: 8 a day
  • GoodShort and StardustTV: 5 a day
  • NetShort: two ads per episode, and the ad carries a three-minute countdown

And then there are free coins, which are the real allowance. ReelShort pays 200 coins for reaching check-in day 7, which covers three episodes on its own. DramaBox pays 50 on the same day. GoodShort’s seven-day chain totals 320 coins, and NetShort’s totals 355 — but NetShort’s expire in seven days.

Which one to pick

Pick DramaBox if you are going to watch a few series and stop. It is the cheapest to start at $2.99, the cheapest per episode at $0.50, and its weekly membership at $12.99 is the lowest renewal of the six.

Pick NetShort if you are going to watch for a year or more. The $99.99 annual pass is about 27 cents a day and nothing else is close. If you only want a week, though, it is the most expensive at $24.99 and the most expensive per episode at 98 coins.

Pick ReelShort if you want to watch as much as possible without paying. On our test title it gave away ten free episodes, unlocks ten a day with ads, and pays 200 coins on day seven. It is the worst yearly buy of the six, so do not plan to stay on a weekly plan here.

Pick ShortMax or GoodShort if you are already in one of them. Both cost $19.99 a week and 50 coins an episode. The difference is in the free allowance and in the app itself: ShortMax shows all three unlock routes on one screen, while GoodShort stages its first run with a preference wall and routes most of its free coins through third-party offerwalls.

Pick StardustTV if you want the widest free window before anything starts charging. It gives 10 episodes free — the most of the six — and it is the only app here with a reading task that pays coins. Just do not plan to stay: no annual pass showed up in the payment flow we saw, so a year looks like 52 weeks of paying.

Before you commit to any of these
  • Check the renewal price on the screen you are paying on. ReelShort quoted $10.99 in the app and $11.87 at its own web checkout, then renewed at $20.51.
  • Every one of them sells through Google Play, so every one of them has to be cancelled in the store. None of the six offers an in-app cancel button.
  • Regions differ. The same 300-coin pack on DramaBox was $2.99 on a US export and ¥410 on a Japan export, on the same account on the same day.

Limits of this comparison

  • One account per app, on Android, in one region, in October 2026. Prices are set by the publishers and move.
  • Free episode counts are samples. The free window varied by series on at least two of the six, so treat every count here as what we saw, not a rule.
  • StardustTV’s annual card was not captured — the payment popup only exposed the weekly tier, so it is the one app we cannot place in the annual column. We did not open every screen, and a plan you did not reach is not the same as a plan that does not exist: ReelShort, for example, hides its small coin packs inside the locked-episode popup rather than on the Store page.
  • NetShort’s coin pack prices are not in the app at all, so its per-episode cost is the one figure on this page we could not calculate.
  • We did not test the TV apps. These are the phone apps.
  • We did not test how well the shows are. This page compares what an episode costs, not whether you will enjoy it. Catalogue depth and episode quality are a separate question, and the honest answer there is that the apps differ a lot in what they carry.
  • When this comparison does not work for you: if you are outside the US, or on a different network route, the app may show a different currency and a different discount — we measured the same DramaBox account at yen and at dollars one day apart. And if you are only watching one short series, none of this ranking matters: buy coins and move on.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best short drama app overall?

It depends on your viewing habit rather than on one app being better. If you finish a series and move on, DramaBox is the cheapest per episode and has the cheapest entry price. If you will watch for months, NetShort at $99.99 a year is the cheapest by a wide margin. If you want to watch as much as possible without paying, ReelShort and StardustTV give away the most. We tested all six on the same account type in October 2026 and found no single winner.

Which short drama app is the cheapest to start with?

DramaBox. Its weekly membership starts at $9.99 for the first three weeks and renews at $12.99, and its smallest coin pack is $2.99 for 300 coins. NetShort starts at $15.99 for one week and renews at $24.99, so the same first episode costs about twice as much there.

Is it worth paying for an annual pass on a short drama app?

Only if you will watch for most of the year. The annual pass is where the ranking flips: NetShort costs $99.99 a year, which works out at about 27 cents a day, while ReelShort costs $199.99, about 55 cents a day. On a monthly basis ReelShort looked far more expensive, but over a full year the gap is roughly two to one rather than the five to one the weekly prices imply. StardustTV showed no annual card in the payment flow we saw, so there is nothing to compare there.

Do any of these apps have a free tier you can keep using?

All six give away some episodes, and the amount varies by series rather than by app. StardustTV gave 10 free episodes of a 50-episode drama, NetShort 9, GoodShort 7, ShortMax 6, DramaBox 11 and ReelShort between 10 and 14 depending on the show. Ad unlocks extend that: ReelShort and ShortMax allow ten a day, DramaBox eight, and GoodShort and StardustTV five.

Why do different websites give different prices for the same app?

Because the apps themselves do. ReelShort quoted us $10.99 for its first week in the app, $11.87 at its own web checkout and $20.51 once it renewed, on the same afternoon. Small coin packs are also hidden: on ReelShort the $4.99 and $9.99 packs only appear inside the locked-episode popup, not on the Store page. Any site quoting a single price for these apps is quoting one screen of a moving target.
