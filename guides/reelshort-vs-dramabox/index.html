ReelShort vs DramaBox: Which Is Cheaper per Episode?

By marluola Published 1 October 2026 Updated 19 October 2026 Prices checked in-app on 2026-10-09
Short answer
  • Per episode, DramaBox is cheaper at both ends of the scale. On the smallest pack it is $0.50 against ReelShort's $0.60; on the largest pack it is $0.36 against $0.40 — a 10% gap.
  • That per-episode gap is small, and it is not the number that should decide it. Finishing one series costs about $30 in ReelShort coins — one $29.99 pack covers both series we finished, against roughly one week of DramaBox membership at $9.99–$12.99 — which covers as many series as you can watch.
  • The honest framing: the two apps are almost identical per episode and wildly different per series, because DramaBox sells a weekly pass and ReelShort sells coins.
  • ReelShort has native English originals with Western casts; DramaBox leans on dubbed and subtitled titles.
  • DramaBox starts charging earlier: we hit its paywall at episode 12 of one series, while ReelShort series ran 10–16 free episodes.
  • Pick by habit: finish about one series a month → ReelShort coins. Two or more → DramaBox subscription.

How we measured this

Every figure on this page comes from one hands-on session per app, not from a review site. The setup was identical throughout:

  • Environment — Android in BlueStacks, real Google Play billing, signed into a US Google Play account so prices rendered in dollars. No VPN region-switching during the pricing pass.
  • Deriving the per-episode price — every app prices locked episodes in coins, not dollars. We read the coin cost per episode from the unlock screen, then divided by the coins-per-dollar of a given pack. That is why the same episode shows two prices on our tables: $0.50 is the entry pack, and the lower figure is what the largest pack works out to. Both are real, and which one applies depends on the pack you buy.
  • Renewal, not intro — where an app offers a discounted first period, we report the renewal price separately. Intro offers in this category carry countdown timers and rotate by day and by account, so they are treated as marketing, not as the price.
  • Confirmed against the store sheet — for every subscription we cross-checked the renewal figure against the Google Play subscription sheet, which is the number that actually reaches the card. Where the in-app text disagreed with the sheet, we report the sheet.

The 30-second answer

This comparison does not resolve cleanly, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something.

Cost: DramaBox wins by a wide margin. Unlimited membership is $9.99/week for the first 3 weeks then $12.99/week (verified in-app on Android, 9 October 2026), and one week covers as many series as you can watch. Finishing a single series with ReelShort coins costs about $30 in one pack.

Production: ReelShort wins on a taste judgement that is widely shared — it films English-language originals with Western casts, so there is no dubbing to get wrong.

The decision comes down to one habit question: how many series do you finish a month?

The cost gap is the whole story

Strip away everything else and the difference is this: on one app you pay per episode, and on the other you pay per week.

Per-episode pricing has no ceiling. At 60 coins per episode and roughly a cent per coin, a 60-episode ReelShort series costs about $30 to finish. That is not a pricing accident — coin economies are designed to convert the moment of maximum narrative investment into a purchase. We work through that mechanism in detail in our price breakdown.

The flip side, which the table below hides: ReelShort’s weekly tier is competitive with DramaBox’s but its annual tier is not. We compare all six apps’ membership plans side by side in the DramaBox membership plan breakdown.

Subscription-first pricing has a ceiling but a different failure mode: it charges you whether you watch or not. DramaBox’s own tips screen states that Google Play will auto-charge within 24 hours before each period ends.

One thing the headline numbers hide: the two apps are close per episode, and which one is cheaper depends on which pack you buy. ReelShort charges 60 coins an episode against DramaBox’s 50, so on the entry pack one episode is $0.60 against $0.50. On the largest packs the gap narrows to its true size: $0.36 on DramaBox against $0.40 on ReelShort — about 10% apart, or four cents.

That is why per-episode arithmetic will not make you switch. The reason to switch is the shape of the pricing underneath it: DramaBox makes the subscription the obvious path, and the subscription is where the gap widens to $20–30 per series. If you do plan to finish series with coins, DramaBox is the cheaper app — just not by much.

So the two apps are actually optimised for opposite viewers:

  • Occasional viewer (one series a month) — coins on ReelShort are a clean, bounded purchase. A subscription would keep charging between series.
  • Regular viewer (two or more a month) — DramaBox’s subscription structure is dramatically cheaper per series, and the ceiling protects you.

There is a second-order effect worth naming, because it is where most of the money actually goes. A subscription you forget to cancel costs more than an expensive purchase you made deliberately. Coin purchases end when you stop buying them; subscriptions do not. That asymmetry means the “expensive” app can end up cheaper if you are bad at cancelling, and the “cheap” app can end up costing more if you are not. Our cancellation walkthrough covers the mechanics for anyone who wants to neutralise that risk.

The mistake to avoid

Choosing by catalogue size or production quality before working out your viewing volume. Both of those are secondary to the pricing shape, because the pricing shape is what determines whether a habit costs you $6 a week or $40 a month.

Where production quality actually differs

The difference is not “good” versus “bad” — it is consistency versus volume.

ReelShort commissions a smaller number of English-language originals shot with real casts. Fewer titles, higher and more even production standard, and no dubbing mismatch because there is no dub.

DramaBox runs the opposite strategy: a much larger catalogue (16,000+ series claimed on its own membership page) with a very fast refresh rate, priced far lower. The trade-off is variance — production quality differs more between titles, and most audio is dubbed rather than performed in English.

The paywall timing differs too, and it matters more than people expect. In our testing, ReelShort series ran 10–16 free episodes before charging (it varies by title, and neither app publishes the boundary). DramaBox hit its paywall at episode 12 of the first series we opened. If you binge before you commit, ReelShort gives you a longer free sample; DramaBox starts monetising sooner but charges less once it does.

How to decide

  1. Use the free tiers on both first. Both give you free opening episodes. Sample the same genre on each and see which one you actually keep watching. This costs nothing and answers the question better than any comparison table. We break down how far each free tier gets you: ReelShort and DramaBox.

  2. Count how many series you finish a month. Be honest — most people overestimate this significantly.

  3. Zero to one series: ReelShort with coins for that specific series — but buy the small packs from the unlock popup, not the store page. Bounded spend, no renewals to manage.

  4. Two or more series: DramaBox. The subscription structure is the cheaper shape for that habit, and the annual tier ($119.99) is $80 cheaper than ReelShort’s if you last that long.

  5. Watch on a television: neither as a first choice — though DramaBox’s bigger grid tolerates a living-room screen better than ReelShort’s portrait originals.

Where this comparison breaks down

  • The price gap is structural, the figures are not. Subscription-first versus coin-first is a permanent difference in shape; the exact dollar amounts are not — both apps run rotating intro offers with 24-hour countdowns. Treat the direction of the gap as reliable and the numbers as a snapshot of 8–9 October 2026.
  • These are US-account Android prices. DramaBox additionally prices by your IP region: the same device and account showed Japanese yen prices when our connection exited in Japan, and dollar prices when it exited in the US. What you see may differ from what we saw.
  • Neither is a good television option. If a TV is your main screen, this whole comparison is largely irrelevant.
  • We cannot judge whether you will like either catalogue. Genre coverage differs as much as volume does, and no table captures that.
  • We earn a commission on both apps. That is why we have written down which one is worse for a given viewer — a comparison that always recommends one app is not a comparison.

Bottom line

DramaBox is cheaper and bigger; ReelShort is better made and better performed in English. Neither is the wrong answer, but picking before you know your monthly viewing volume is. Sample both on the free tiers, count your habit honestly, and the choice makes itself.

Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper per episode, ReelShort or DramaBox?

DramaBox, on both pack sizes. ReelShort charges 60 coins per episode against DramaBox's 50, so on the smallest pack one episode is $0.60 against $0.50. On the largest pack ReelShort's 50% bonus takes it to $0.40 while DramaBox's larger pack on a cheaper base price reaches $0.36. In short: $0.36 against $0.40 on best-rate arithmetic, roughly 10% apart.

Is ReelShort or DramaBox cheaper?

DramaBox, and by much more than the per-episode figures suggest. Verified in-app on Android (October 2026): DramaBox unlimited membership is $9.99/week for the first 3 weeks then $12.99/week ($119.99/year, about $2.31/week), and one week covers multiple full series. Finishing the same series with ReelShort coins costs about $30 in one pack at 60 coins per episode.

Which is cheaper for finishing a whole series?

DramaBox by a wide margin. Per episode the two apps are close — $0.36 against $0.40 at best rates — but DramaBox sells a weekly membership while ReelShort sells coins, and coins have no ceiling. About $9.99–$12.99 of DramaBox membership covers as many series as you can watch; the same series costs about $30 in ReelShort coins — one $29.99 pack covered both series we finished.

Which has better content quality?

ReelShort has more consistent per-title production quality because it commissions English-language originals with real casts. DramaBox has a much larger catalogue, but quality varies more between titles and most audio is dubbed rather than performed in English.

Which has more shows?

DramaBox claims 16,000+ series with 100+ new titles every week (stated on its own membership page). ReelShort keeps a smaller, more curated catalogue with a higher average production standard.

Can I use both?

Yes, and for some viewers that is the sensible answer — use the free tiers on both to sample broadly, then pay only on the one whose catalogue actually holds your attention.
