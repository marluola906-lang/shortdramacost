What we ran into while testing

  • The Top Up page listed only the two passes — no coin packs at all. We could not convert 98 N coins into a dollar cost without pack prices, so we did not invent one.
  • A brand-new account started with 55 N coins. One episode costs 98 N, so a first-time user cannot unlock a single episode with what they are given.
  • The Google Play subscription sheet matched the in-app price exactly ($15.99 intro, $24.99 renewal) — the most consistent disclosure of the apps we tested. We still saw five different weekly prices elsewhere in the same session.
  • The unlock ad ran to three minutes — the longest forced ad in our testing — and the ad route requires two ads per episode rather than one.
  • Everything above was captured in an emulator with the real Google Play billing SDK, so the prices we could capture are real, not estimated.

98 coins per episode

NetShort’s per-episode unlock is 98 N coins — the steepest single price we recorded across all six apps in this comparison. The rest of the category sits at 50–65 coins per episode; NetShort is nearly double.

Every price on this page was read inside the app on an Android emulator running the real Google Play billing SDK, on 9 October 2026, in US dollars.

The important caveat is not the size of the number — it is that we could not turn it into a dollar figure. Here is why.

The price the app will not show

This is the part worth knowing before you subscribe.

NetShort’s Top Up page lists only the two passes — the annual and the weekly. It shows no coin packs at all.

Without pack prices, there is no way to convert “98 N coins” into dollars before you subscribe. We are not going to estimate a per-coin rate from another app and pretend it applies here. The honest statement is:

We cannot give you a per-episode dollar cost for NetShort, and neither can the app

Most apps in this category show their coin packs on the top-up screen, so you can work out the per-episode cost before paying. NetShort does not — the packs are absent. That is not a missing detail on our side; it is how the app presents itself. If you need the coin route, open the Top Up page on your own account; it may differ by region or account age. We report only what we could verify.

A new user cannot unlock one episode

A first-time account starts with 55 N coins in welcome bonus. One episode costs 98 N. So a new user who hits the paywall on episode 10 cannot unlock a single episode with what they are given — they must subscribe or buy coins (and, per above, the coin price is not shown).

That is a harder wall than any other app we tested: elsewhere the new-user balance at least covers one or two episodes.

The free route and its catches

On the 57-episode series we tested, episodes 1–9 were free — the second-widest opening we measured. After that:

  • Unlocking costs 98 N coins, or
  • Two ads per episode (double what ReelShort and DramaBox ask), with no published daily cap, and
  • One of our unlock ads ran for three minutes — the longest forced ad in our testing.

The welcome bonus (link a messaging app or email, enable notifications) and a 7-day check-in (355 N total) help, but bonus coins expire after seven days and the check-in itself is gated behind watching an ad.

What this page does not cover

The central limitation of this page is the missing coin-pack price. We could not price the coin route because NetShort does not publish coin packs in the top-up flow we saw. The per-episode dollar cost is therefore unknown to both us and the app’s own UI. We also saw five different weekly prices in one session ($7.99 / $14.99 / $14.39 / $15.99 / $24.99) — only the Play-sheet renewal ($24.99) describes what you keep paying. Treat all figures as dated measurements (9 October 2026).

Bottom line

NetShort charges 98 N coins per episode — the most expensive per-episode unlock of the six apps — and its top-up page shows no coin packs, so the dollar cost cannot be worked out before subscribing. A new account’s 55 N balance cannot unlock even one episode. The offset is a $99.99 annual pass, the cheapest year in the comparison, with weekly renewals at $24.99. If you commit a year, NetShort wins on price; if you watch one episode at a time, it is the most expensive and the least transparent option here.
