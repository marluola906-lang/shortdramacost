Does ShortMax Support TV? What We Could Confirm

By marluola Published 18 October 2026 Updated 18 October 2026 Prices checked in-app on 2026-10-09
Short answer
  • Yes, ShortMax is listed with TV apps — separate builds for Android TV and Fire TV. ReelShort, by contrast, has no dedicated TV build that we could find; it is phone and tablet plus a web player.
  • This is a listing fact, not a tested one. We installed ShortMax on Android in BlueStacks and captured its pricing screens; we did not install the TV build, so we cannot confirm the interface, playback quality or whether purchases work on a television.
  • What we did verify on the phone build: three unlock routes on the paywall (50 coins / watch an ad / subscribe), 6 free episodes before the lock in the series we opened, and a 10-per-day ad-unlock cap.
  • ReelShort is built vertically for a phone, so even with casting it does not fill a television well. ShortMax at least has a native television build.
  • The TV app does not change the pricing: the same $19.99 weekly and $149.99 annual tiers apply, so watch on a TV for free and decide about paying from the phone.

What we ran into while testing

  • We saw ShortMax prices without signing in at all. The number was on the screen the moment the app opened.
  • ShortMax handed a new account 100 bonus coins. The unlock screen then flashed 'Auto Pay 50 Bonus' afterwards, which we never got to the bottom of.
  • The ad that opened on ShortMax was a full-screen Temu install. It is the same quality tier as the fake insurance ad we hit on ReelShort.
  • The ShortMax paywall offers all three routes on one screen: watch an ad, spend 50 coins, or subscribe.
How we measured this

Every figure on this page comes from one hands-on session per app, not from a review site. The setup was identical throughout:

  • Environment — Android in BlueStacks, real Google Play billing, signed into a US Google Play account so prices rendered in dollars. No VPN region-switching during the pricing pass.
  • Deriving the per-episode price — every app prices locked episodes in coins, not dollars. We read the coin cost per episode from the unlock screen, then divided by the coins-per-dollar of a given pack. That is why the same episode shows two prices on our tables: $0.50 is the entry pack, and the lower figure is what the largest pack works out to. Both are real, and which one applies depends on the pack you buy.
  • Renewal, not intro — where an app offers a discounted first period, we report the renewal price separately. Intro offers in this category carry countdown timers and rotate by day and by account, so they are treated as marketing, not as the price.
  • Confirmed against the store sheet — for every subscription we cross-checked the renewal figure against the Google Play subscription sheet, which is the number that actually reaches the card. Where the in-app text disagreed with the sheet, we report the sheet.

The short answer

ShortMax is listed with TV apps. ReelShort is not.

That is the finding, and it is worth stating plainly at the top: we confirmed it from app listings, and we did not install the television build. So treat this as “the app exists for your television”, not as “we have seen it work”.

For most people the practical answer is simpler than it sounds: you can watch both apps on a television, but only one of them was built for it. ShortMax has a native Android TV and Fire TV build. ReelShort has a web player you can cast or open on a TV browser, and nothing more.

Saying so is more useful than padding it out — if you need to know whether ShortMax’s TV build will actually let you subscribe from your sofa, we cannot tell you, and we would rather admit that than guess.

What ReelShort offers instead

ReelShort’s position is different in kind rather than degree.

It has a web player, which means you can open ReelShort in a browser on any television with a browser and cast or connect it. It does not have a dedicated Android TV or Fire TV build, and we could not find one.

The important caveat is about framing, and it is not a small one. ReelShort films its originals vertically for a phone. Putting a tall narrow strip onto a 55-inch screen does not make it more watchable — it makes it smaller than the phone you were already watching it on. Casting solves the problem of getting the picture to the television; it does not solve the problem of a portrait frame on a landscape display.

Tablets have the same issue. They work, and they waste screen space.

What this means in practice

  1. If your main screen is a television, ShortMax is the only one of these two apps built for it. A native TV build means remote navigation that was designed for the job, rather than a mirrored phone screen.

  2. Check the listing on your own device first. TV app availability varies by store and by region, and Fire TV in particular has a different store from Android TV. We cannot tell you what will appear on your television.

  3. Use the free tiers on the phone before paying anything. Six free episodes and 10 ad-unlocks a day is enough to find out whether the catalogue holds your attention. Spending $19.99 before you have seen three episodes is backwards.

  4. If you do subscribe, do it on the phone. Subscription management is much easier in the mobile app, and the cancellation routes differ between the two apps. Our cancellation walkthrough covers why that matters.

  5. Do not expect a TV build to fix portrait video. A native television app solves navigation, not framing. If a tall narrow video is a problem for you, casting a vertical video to a landscape television does not solve it either.

Where our testing breaks down

  • We never installed the TV build. Everything in the confirmed table above about ShortMax’s TV app comes from store listings, not from use. Interface, playback, remote navigation and — most importantly — whether purchases complete on a television are all untested.
  • Our ShortMax testing was Android phone only, on a US Google Play account inside BlueStacks. iOS and Fire TV builds may behave differently.
  • The free-episode figure is one title. Six free episodes out of 30 in the series we opened. No app in this category publishes its policy.
  • Casting is device-dependent. Whether ReelShort’s web player appears on your television depends on your hardware, not on the app, and we did not test any casting setup.
  • We earn a commission on both apps. We list both CTAs because both are legitimately relevant to a television question, not because we are ranking them against each other here.

Bottom line

ShortMax has a TV app; ReelShort does not, only a web player. That is ShortMax’s one genuine structural advantage over ReelShort, and it matters if a television is your main screen.

What we cannot tell you is whether the TV build is any good, because we did not test it. Check the listing on your own device, watch the free episodes on your phone first, and only then decide whether $19.99 a week is worth it.

Frequently asked questions

Does ShortMax have a TV app?

ShortMax is listed with dedicated TV apps for Android TV and Fire TV. We did not install or test that build, so we can confirm the listing but not the experience. What we tested was the Android phone app, where three unlock routes appear on the paywall: 50 coins, watch an advertisement, or subscribe.

Can I watch ShortMax on a Fire TV Stick?

ShortMax is listed as available on Fire TV, which is the stick you most likely mean. We have not tested it on a physical device, so we cannot report on performance, remote navigation or whether in-app purchases complete on a television. Check the app listing on your device's store before assuming it will install.

Does ReelShort work on a TV?

ReelShort has a web player in addition to its phone and tablet apps, which is the practical way to get it onto a television. We could not find a dedicated TV build for Android TV or Fire TV. Tablets work but waste screen space on vertical video, and televisions are not properly supported.

Is ShortMax free on TV?

The free routes are the same on any device: 6 free episodes in the series we opened, 10 ad-unlocks a day, and a 100-coin welcome bonus. The TV app does not add a separate free tier, and we have not checked whether ad unlocks work the same way on a television.

Why do these apps look bad on a television?

Because the content is shot vertically for a phone. ReelShort is the clearest case — its originals are phone-first portrait video, so even on a large screen you are watching a tall narrow strip. A native TV build will not fix framing that was shot that way; it fixes navigation and remote control, nothing more.
